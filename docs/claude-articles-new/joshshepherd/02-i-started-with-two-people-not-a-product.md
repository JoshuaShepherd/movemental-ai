# I Started with Two People, Not a Product

By then I had spent close to three years using this technology in front of people, all of it free, all of it alongside a full time job. Ben first, then a team from Honeywell, then a philanthropy group, then Youthfront, then movement leaders.

The obvious move with that experience is to pick a market and build for it. I did not, for a reason I would defend and one I would not.

The one I would defend is that I do not think you learn what somebody needs by studying the category they belong to. I ran an intentional community for eleven years before any of this. You learn what a person needs by being in the room with them, in their language, for a long time.

The one I would not defend is that I did not think about money at all until I decided to start a company. That is not discipline. It is just how I am built, and it happened to work out, because a market built on trust was not open yet and would not be for another two years.

So I built for two situations I actually knew.

#### The first. A body of work in pieces.

Search for the questions Alan Hirsch has spent his life answering and you will mostly find other people restating him. Some of them get it right. Some do not, and the ones who do not are frequently the easier read, so they travel further.

Twenty thousand notes. More than a decade of them. Books, talks, half finished thoughts, things written on planes. The work was all there. It was not anywhere you could get to.

The problem was never storage. Anyone can put twenty thousand notes in a database.

The problem is that the moment you make an archive answerable, you have built something that speaks in a person's name. If it says something he would not say, you have not helped him. You have added one more confident wrong voice to the pile that was the original problem.

So the build had two requirements that pull against each other. It had to actually answer. And it had to only ever say things he had said.

A hundred hours is a lot to ask of a nonprofit and I am not going to dress that up. It is the most expensive thing about how I work and I have not found a way around it that I believe in.

#### Why the hundred hours exists

This is the part I would skip if I were trying to make the work sound easier, so here it is.

For three years I showed this technology to everyone I knew, for free, as well as I know how to show anything. People were astonished and people did not adopt. They called me a wizard. They moved their schedules to watch me work. They passed it around. And then nothing.

My best friend, who I prepared personally by spending five days learning his profession so the demo would sit inside his actual work, was impressed and did not touch AI again for two years.

I read that as a failure in people for a long time. I do not anymore. It is the fastest spreading technology in history and also the hardest one to actually grasp. It looks like a party trick, so you try it, it makes a mistake, and some part of you is relieved. Or you sit in front of the empty box and cannot think what to ask. Or you can, and the back and forth is genuinely tiring and looks like nothing from outside.

It was not that people could not. It was too early. It is just starting now.

But the result stands, and it is the most expensive thing I know: **astonishment is not adoption, and a demonstration changes nobody.** Three years of the most patient demonstration I could run produced almost no adopted practice anywhere.

That is why the training is a hundred hours and why it is in person. Not generosity. Findings.

#### Where the two halves met

I did not set out to build a platform. I set out to solve two problems for people I knew, and they turned out to be the same problem in different clothes.

Alan's problem was a body of work scattered into pieces the world cannot reassemble. Youthfront's problem was AI arriving before anyone decided what it was allowed to touch. In both cases something was about to speak in their name, authoritatively, with nobody having decided what it could say.

The tool half without the training half hands an organization a capable machine and no formed judgment about it. The training half without the tool half gives you careful people and nothing to be careful with. I had watched both fail.

It also became clear that building for leaders and building for organizations is the same work. Leaders come first only because a leader can decide on a Tuesday and an organization takes a year.

Between September 2025 and April 2026 I built the platform, the agents, and the system underneath, working in context with Cursor and Claude Code, alongside a full time job for most of it. In March 2026 I went full time.

#### What I got wrong on the way

I had been building front ends fast enough that I assumed a full stack application would work the same way. It does not, and I walked straight into it.

A week into building a custom agentic CRM for the company I was working for, I gave a demo. It was bad. Not close. And I had spent two years training that room to expect a mobile app in a week.

Within days I went back and said we needed real timelines and real expectations, and we set them. What took months after that was not diagnosis. It was learning back end architecture from a standing start. Data, type safety, migrations, how an application actually holds and moves information. There was no map for that in 2025 and there still barely is.

I delivered in about six weeks against a three month timeline. It did not matter, because the room had decided in week one.

Two things came out of it. One is a rule I now keep: I do not test in public. I show it when it works. From outside there is no visible difference between this is hard and this was oversold, and I watch capable people hit that same wall constantly, including my own co-founders.

The other is the thing I would not trade. The same day it became clear I had to build a real data chain was the day it became clear I could build one from the prompt level. That idea is underneath everything I have made since, including the design work.

Sometimes you say you will be back in a week and it turns out to be a longer trip, and you come back with a map nobody had.

#### What it proved

**Building for one real person beats building for a category.** Everything good in the platform came from a specific problem Alan or Youthfront actually had. Everything I built from a guess about what leaders in general would want, I threw out.

**A grounded answer is worth more than a fluent one.** Fluency is cheap now and it is about to be free. What will still be scarce in five years is an answer you can check, in a voice the person would claim.

**The training is not the soft part.** It is what the build is for. A tool without a formed person is not progress. It is replacement, and it arrives looking like help.

## 4. Shrewd as serpents

### Shrewd as serpents

*For church leaders. ~2,600 words.*

---

There is a line in Luke that I have not been able to get out of my head for about two years now.

Jesus tells a story about a manager who is about to be fired. Before the books close, the man goes to everyone who owes his master money and reduces what they owe, so that when he is out of a job there will be people willing to take him in. And then the master commends him. Not for his honesty, which Luke pointedly does not grant him. For his shrewdness.

Then Jesus says the thing. The children of this age are shrewder in dealing with their own generation than the children of light.

I have been in and around ministry for most of my adult life, and I have spent the last three and a half years watching what churches and Christian organizations do when a genuinely new technology arrives. I do not think that sentence has ever been more plainly true than it is right now.

#### What I have actually watched

Since December 2022 I have shown this technology to church leaders, denominational staff, nonprofit teams, seminary people, and about every kind of ministry organization there is. Most of it for free, most of it alongside a full time job.

The pattern almost never varies. People are impressed. And then nothing happens.

Sometimes nothing happens because the leader has decided this is a threat, and that waiting is the faithful posture. Sometimes nothing happens because the leader is enthusiastic but has no idea what the next step would be. And sometimes something does happen, but it happens sideways: nobody decides anything at the top, so the youth director and the communications person and the executive assistant each quietly figure it out on their own accounts, at night, using it for the tasks that are hardest to write, which are exactly the tasks that involve real people.

Two failures, opposite in appearance. One organization is paralyzed. The other is unsupervised. Neither has deliberated.

What strikes me is that Scripture has a word for each of them, and it is not the word most of us reach for.

#### Both halves of the command

When Jesus sends the twelve out, he tells them they are sheep among wolves, and then gives them a two-part instruction that most of us can quote and few of us hold together.

Be shrewd as serpents. Be unmixed as doves.

Start with the first half, because it should be more shocking than it is. The serpent is not a neutral image in the Hebrew Scriptures. The word used of the serpent in Genesis 3 is the same word Proverbs uses over and over for the prudent person, the one who sees trouble coming and takes cover while the naive walk straight into it. One word, condemned in Eden and commended in Proverbs.

The tradition never resolved that by getting rid of the faculty. It resolved it by locating the moral weight in what the craft is aimed at. Cunning aimed at deceiving is serpentine. The identical cunning aimed at protecting the people in your care is wisdom.

So when Jesus says be shrewd as serpents, he is reaching for the most negatively charged picture of cleverness available in his own Scriptures and telling his people to match it.

The second half is where most of us have quietly changed the meaning. The Greek word behind "innocent" does not mean naive. It means unmixed, unalloyed, the way metal is described when nothing has been added to it. It is a word about purity of composition, not about being uninformed.

Which means the command is not be clever but also a little bit stupid. It is: **be maximally competent in your means and completely unadulterated in your motives.**
