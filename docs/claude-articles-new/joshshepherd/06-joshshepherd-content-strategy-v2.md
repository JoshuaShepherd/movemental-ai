Artifact 02 · positioning

## Two properties, reframed

### joshshepherd.ai

Your vocational home.

  * **Purpose:** the person at the intersection, and the body of work that comes from it. Movemental is one expression of the calling.
  * **Promise:** _open — this is the one-line you're developing with me._
  * **For:** builders, peers, movement leaders leading with AI, the person checking who's behind Movemental, and the few who'd back or book you.
  * **Not:** the company site; a funnel wearing your name; polish that hides the person.
  * **Voice:** singular — _"I."_

### movemental.ai

The company and the method.

  * **Purpose:** explain the thesis, convert organizations onto the Path, house the proof and the network.
  * **For:** churches, nonprofits, institutions, prospective Voices.
  * **Voice:** plural — _"we,"_ fronted by Brad as the trust seat.

**The seam — now a benefit, not the point.** The trust-check still happens: someone vetting Movemental crosses over to see the human behind it. But it's _more_ believable here precisely because this site isn't built to sell them. Vocational-first delivers the instrumental payoff better than an instrumental site would.

Artifact 04 · voice — open workstream

## How you sound (to be derived, not assumed)

The markers below are a strong working draft from your record. But a full voice description is its own workstream, and yours is non-standard: you have no shelf of published books to derive it from. Your corpus is your **builds, thesis docs, field guides, and conversations like this one** — so voice gets derived from those.

### Markers (working)

  * Plain sentences. Short. No em dashes.
  * Concede the other side first.
  * Name the threat, then sell relief.
  * Lead with the gift in the hard thing.
  * Picturable noun over abstract category.
  * Bold claim, then invite scrutiny. Name who it's not for.

### Refusals

  * No manufactured urgency or scarcity.
  * No faked authorship — AI never writes the word under your name.
  * AI never touches discernment or care.
  * No unearned claim; extra care with vulnerable people.

Artifact 03 · the spine

## The story — this is the homepage, not the footnote

Assembled from your own words across past sessions. For a vocational home, this narrative is the center of gravity: it's simultaneously who you are, what you think, and proof of what you can do. It's yours to make public or hold — the candid parts are marked.

The frame

### The intersection

You work where AI meets the people and communities whose work runs on trust — and you help each meet the other without losing what's true. That's not "builder-founder." It's a bridge, and it grows in both directions at once.

Roots

### Formation you can't automate

Bilingual, frontline service to immigrant families in Kansas City. Called to ministry in 2007. Then, with your wife, a neomonastic community — extended residency alongside young adults, sharing meals, money, prayer, and life.

"That's where I learned that formation happens in relationship, in presence, over time. It can't be faked, and it can't be automated."

The pivot

### Learning to build

You learned digital marketing to tell the community's story — the craft came second to the purpose. Then, at SpecChem, you became a serious builder and proved, on real deadlines, that one person with AI can build what used to take a team and a quarter-million dollars.

The hinge

### Two things at once

Late 2025, the cost of building software collapsed almost overnight.

"Two things hit me at once: that AI was about to do real damage to the things I care most about — credibility, authorship, the slow work of formation — and that, for the first time, someone with my background could actually build the infrastructure to protect them."

The beginning

### Two people, not a product

You started building for Alan Hirsch — the voice half, letting a lifetime of thinking survive contact with AI without being faked or flattened. And for Youthfront — the formation half, training a whole organization to use these tools with discernment instead of fear.

"Two halves of the same thing. And lately those two halves have started converging into one. That's the part I couldn't have planned."

The conviction

### Redeeming the tool

The whole point: use the very technology that threatens credibility, authorship, and formation to protect them instead. AI as the infrastructure around the writing, never the writing itself — the mirror that lets a scattered movement see itself whole.

Candid · your call to make public

### What you lie awake on

The collapse of identity into vocation — the alignment between your life and this work is unusually perfect, which is exactly the condition under which a person can stop telling the calling from the self. And the diversity question: whether this gets built for the unseen, or quietly defaults to the people already within reach. _People are pastored, not processed._

**The work here:** assemble this into the site's About / home narrative and decide, with me, how much of the candid and faith material goes public (see Boundaries). The raw material is already yours and already written.

Artifact 11 · content plan · what to make

## What to make

Each type opens to a full prescription — what it is, where it lives, what it must accomplish, the elements it requires, and how it cascades out. Tap any row.

**How I built it** SAY·FIND·MOVE · flagship · all personas

+

What it is A first-person account of a real build — the two-people origin, the Alan platform, the solo seven-month build — told as narrative, not tutorial. It carries the arc, the thinking, the proof, and the résumé in one artifact. This is the form only you can make, and the reason to visit.

Where it lives The flagship hub (`/how-i-built-it`), each piece on its own canonical URL. The two or three strongest anchor the homepage.

What it must accomplish Prove what you think and can do without a pitch; teach builders as a side effect; give the checker and the leader evidence of both competence and character; seed the FIND/GEO layer so you become the answer on "one person built X with AI."

Length & format 1,200–2,200 words · 6–10 min read. One hero build-image; an optional 60–90s screen-recorded demo embedded. First person throughout.

Core components (ordered) 1 — Opening scene: the real problem, in one picturable moment. 2 — The stakes: why it mattered (the conviction under it). 3 — The build: what you did, told chronologically, the key choices, and the one hard part that broke. 4 — The proof: one checkable result — a number, a link, a before/after, a capture. 5 — The turn: the thesis it demonstrates. 6 — The close: a question that invites thinking-alongside, not a CTA. 7 — Provenance: how AI was used, plainly.

Rubric of execution Lead with the scene, not the setup. One build = one story — never cram two. The wrinkle is the credibility, so keep the hard part honest. Proof must be verifiable, not asserted ("grew a lot" fails). Keep the teaching implicit: a builder should be able to follow it and a leader should feel the conviction without either being lectured. Plain voice, short sentences, no em dashes. You run the company that argues for verifiability — every build claim here is true and checkable. _Failure modes:_ tutorial-dry, brag-without-wrinkle, vague proof, the human buried under the tech.

Creative choices Foreground the human story (Alan / Youthfront) _or_ the technical feat (seven-month solo) by audience: leaders and checkers → human; builders and backers → feat. Include a code/architecture snippet only in builder-heavy pieces. Let the demo clip lead or merely support. Recommended default: human-forward, one technical detail, demo supporting.

Cascade LinkedIn narrative excerpt + link (your warm network lives here) → a short build-in-public thread → a 60–90s screen-recorded demo clip to YouTube + LinkedIn native → featured in the next newsletter → the demo embedded in the AI Lab or a case page.

**Essay (thinking)** SAY · peer · leader · checker

+

What it is Your authored argument on one pillar idea — fragmentation, authorship, formation, legibility. The SAY layer at depth: the thinking the "how I built it" pieces demonstrate.

Where it lives The thinking hub; each essay is a pillar or cluster page, interlinked hub-and-spoke so a reader can move through the body of work.

What it must accomplish State a point of view sharp enough to be cited and argued with; anchor the thesis; over time, become the machine-returned answer on these topics.

Length & format Three tiers. **Pillar** (the definitional anchor of a theme) 3,500–5,000 words · ~20 min. **Cluster** (a deepening) 2,000–3,000 words · ~12 min. **Note** (a single sharp provocation) 1,000–1,800 words · ~6 min. Show reading time.

Core components (from the nine-section spine) 1 — Hook / reframing question. 2 — Definition anchor: the key term in plain language (the FIND surface; must stand alone as a quotable answer). 3 — Why it matters now. 4 — The core argument, strongest counter-case conceded first. 5 — What this is _not_ (common misunderstandings). 6 — At least one picturable example or case. 7 — The formation invitation: something to do or reconsider. 8 — How this connects (links to sibling pieces / the pillar). Optional FAQ block for search-shaped questions.

Rubric of execution Say the true thing first — never open from search demand. The definition anchor must be liftable verbatim by an answer engine. Concede before you counter. One picturable noun beats three abstractions. End by moving the reader (at minimum: dissonance + one next action), not just informing. Sources named at the foot; no invented quotes. Hold vocabulary steady across essays — same term, same meaning — because that consistency is what makes the whole body of work legible to people and machines. _Failure modes:_ throat-clearing intros, abstraction stacking, confirming what the reader already thinks, unsourced assertions.

Creative choices Pick the tier by role: pillar to anchor a theme, cluster to deepen, note for a single take. How personal to run it — measured argument vs first-person conviction; your vocational home tilts personal. Include the FAQ block when the topic carries real search-shaped questions. One signature "reframe" move per essay, where the structure calls for it — not more.

Cascade Newsletter feature → LinkedIn post carrying the core claim + link → 2–3 quotable lines pulled for social → a strong essay seeds a podcast talking point.

**Newsletter** MOVE · all · the owned relationship

+

What it is The owned, algorithm-proof relationship — a regular, plain letter to the people who chose to hear from you. The one channel no platform can take away.

Where it lives Your list (a simple ESP or Substack-style tool — platform TBD), with every issue archived on the site at a public URL.

What it must accomplish Keep a warm relationship independent of any platform; carry each new piece to the people most likely to act; be the destination everything else points to.

Length & format 400–900 words · 2–4 min. Plain-text feel over heavy design. One primary link, one primary image at most.

Core components (ordered) 1 — A short authored opening: a real thought, what's on your mind (2–4 sentences — this is what earns the open). 2 — The feature: the new piece framed in one paragraph (why it matters, not a summary). 3 — One or two secondary links (older work, or a peer's — the scenius move). 4 — One clear next step: read, reply, or share. 5 — A human sign-off.

Rubric of execution The opening earns the click — make it a thought, never "here's my new post." Frame, don't summarize (leave a curiosity gap). One primary CTA only. Write to one person, not a list. Cadence over polish: a plain letter sent reliably beats a designed one sent rarely. Archive every issue at a public URL so it also feeds FIND. _Failure modes:_ newsletter-as-changelog, competing CTAs, over-design, irregularity.

Creative choices Cadence — every two weeks is the recommended floor; weekly only if volume supports it without strain. Occasionally write original short-form _in_ the letter (a "thinking out loud" issue) rather than always featuring a site piece — the mix keeps it alive. Reply-driven (end on a real question) vs broadcast; reply-driven builds the relationship faster.

Cascade It _is_ the owned destination. Every page and every social bio carries a signup. Each issue's public archive URL feeds FIND. LinkedIn posts periodically invite subscribes.

**Build-in-public note** SAY·MOVE · builder · peer

+

What it is Short, in-the-moment posts as you ship — the smaller, faster sibling of "how I built it." Presence between the big pieces, and the raw material they're later assembled from.

Where it lives Primarily LinkedIn and X (rented), collected on the site as a notes stream.

What it must accomplish Show the work as it happens; keep a steady heartbeat; feed the eventual flagship narratives.

Length & format 80–250 words, social-native. One image, screenshot, or snippet. A thread (3–5 beats) only when the thing genuinely has 3–5 beats — never manufactured.

Core components (ordered) 1 — The one concrete thing you did or learned (specificity is the hook). 2 — A visual: screenshot, snippet, before/after. 3 — The honest wrinkle (what was hard or surprising). 4 — A light link home, only when it truly adds.

Rubric of execution Specific beats clever — "I cut retrieval latency by moving X" outlaps "thoughts on AI." Show, don't announce. One idea per note. The wrinkle is the value — people follow builders for the real parts. Don't force a CTA; presence is the point. _Failure modes:_ vague musings, humblebrags, over-linking, threads spun from a single thought.

Creative choices Platform emphasis — LinkedIn for the warm ministry/founder network now; add X only if you deliberately build a dev audience later. How raw to go is a Boundaries decision (client / Movemental-internal exposure). Cadence is as-you-ship, not scheduled.

Cascade Native social first → batch a month of notes into a newsletter roundup or fold them into a longer "how I built it" piece.

**Podcast / guest** FIND·MOVE · peer · leader

+

What it is Appearances on others' shows (guest first; hosting only if ever), borrowing trusted hosts' networks — starting inside Brad's and Alan's orbit, which is where your audience already is.

Where it lives Their platforms; the episode embedded and transcribed on your site.

What it must accomplish Reach warm-adjacent audiences through people who already vouch for you; discovery plus intimacy; and every appearance becomes an article.

Length & format The appearance is the host's — typically 30–60 min. Your asset is a one-page talking map. The derived article runs 1,500–2,500 words; clips are 30–90s, 2–4 per episode.

Core components (your prep + follow-through) 1 — Your 2–3 core stories, tight (the two-people origin, one how-I-built-it, one conviction). 2 — The thesis in three spoken sentences. 3 — 3–5 "bridge" lines to get from any question back to your point. 4 — One clear "where to find me" — the newsletter. 5 — Post-show: transcript → article, clips, a newsletter mention, within the week.

Rubric of execution Tell stories, don't recite arguments — audio rewards narrative. Leave one takeaway the host can restate in their own words. Name the newsletter once, naturally. Be generous to the host's audience: teach, don't pitch. Repurpose while it's warm. _Failure modes:_ rambling, jargon, no clear "find me," letting the episode die un-repurposed.

Creative choices Which stories to lead with, per the show's audience. Pre-write the derived article vs transcribe-then-edit — transcribe-then-edit keeps your spoken voice and is recommended. Guest-only now vs hosting later: stay guest-first — borrow networks before building one.

Cascade Transcript → article on your site → clips to YouTube + LinkedIn → featured in the newsletter → the host's audience routed to your list.

**Selected work / proof** SAY (proof) · backer · checker

+

What it is The résumé layer — the builds and their results, gathered and checkable. Not narrative like "how I built it"; fast, scannable evidence.

Where it lives A `/work` page, with individual case pages for the biggest builds.

What it must accomplish Give the backer and the checker fast, verifiable proof; anchor the credibility that quietly backs Movemental.

Length & format The index is scannable — a grid, ~40–60 words per entry. Full case pages for the biggest 3–5 builds run 600–1,200 words with one visual each.

Core components Per index entry: 1 — what it was (one line). 2 — your role, precisely (built / led / advised). 3 — the result: a number, a link, a checkable artifact. 4 — a one-line "why it mattered." Case pages add: the context, the key decision, the outcome, a visual.

Rubric of execution Honest over impressive — inflation is the one thing that kills this page for the checker. Numbers carry sources. Name your actual role exactly; the whole site argues for verifiable authorship, so practice it hardest here. Link to live proof wherever it exists. _Failure modes:_ vague superlatives, unclear role, unlinked claims, too many entries diluting the strong ones.

Creative choices How many entries — curate hard; 6–10 strong beats 20 mixed. Which get full case pages — the 3–5 that best serve the backer and checker. Whether to show client logos/names — a Boundaries + permission decision.

Cascade Mostly a destination, not a broadcast. Linked from the About/story, from every "how I built it" piece, and from your LinkedIn "Featured" section.
