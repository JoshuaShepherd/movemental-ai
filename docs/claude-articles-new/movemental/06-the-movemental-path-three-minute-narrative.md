# The Movemental Path: Three-Minute Narrative


**With AI, decided deliberately.** Half a page of plain sentences about what the organization will and will not do, read aloud to staff, argued with, and revised. Most organizations can do this themselves in a quarter without hiring anyone.

---

#### 9. Institutional knowledge in one person

An operations manager has been at an organization for nineteen years. She knows which donor cannot be called in December, why a program runs on Tuesdays, and which partner will say yes if asked face to face. None of it is written down. She retires next year.

**Without AI.** Someone interviews her, transcribes it, organizes it, and keeps it current. It is a real project competing against this week's work, and it loses. New staff spend their first year rediscovering things the organization already knew.

**With AI.** Recording and transcription make capture cheap enough to actually complete, and a searchable store makes the result retrievable rather than filed. The interviews still have to happen and somebody still has to ask good questions.

---

#### 10. A year to become useful

A new communications hire spends her first year learning how things are done — the voice, the standards, the reasons behind decisions made before she arrived. She asks a senior colleague roughly four questions a day, most of which have documented answers somewhere.

**Without AI.** Knowledge transfers by proximity: sitting near people, asking, being corrected. It works and it is slow, and it caps how fast an organization can grow to how much its senior people can supervise.

**With AI.** The same context, written down once and made askable, so she can find out how the last version of this was handled without waiting for someone's Tuesday. The watching and correcting still happen. The repeated questions stop.

---

#### 11. The grant report

A program director has a mid-grant report due in ten days. The summer happened, it went well, and almost none of it was written down as it occurred. She reconstructs week three from a calendar and two group chats, guesses at attendance, and under-claims work that genuinely took place.

**Without AI.** Somebody documents as the program runs. In a small organization that person is also driving the van, so it does not happen. The report under-represents the work, which affects renewal and the next application.

**With AI.** A voice note at the end of each day, transcribed, kept against the grant's deliverables as the season goes. At the end the report is assembled from records made at the time, and the person writing it is checking rather than remembering.

---

#### 12. No position to state

A board chair asks what the organization's position on AI is. Nobody has a sentence to read aloud. Half the staff use it daily. The item is minuted as something to look into and does not return for a year.

**Without AI.** Commission a policy from a consultant who has not been in the building, receive a document, file it. Or write nothing, which means each staff member decides individually.

**With AI.** Research the organization's own public material and existing commitments, draft the layers — a statement, a policy, the rules, and what to do when something goes wrong — and put the draft in front of the people who have to live with it. The draft is the fast part. The argument about it is the work.

---

#### 13. Three versions of the same program

A development officer, a communications lead, and a program director each describe the same program in the same month. The three descriptions differ on what it is for, who it serves, and how many people attend. A funder, a parent, and a volunteer each receive a different organization.

**Without AI.** One person owns the canonical language and enforces it across departments, which requires authority nobody quite holds, and drifts again within a year.
