# 22 Problem Stories: Without AI vs. With AI


**With AI.** One searchable source of what the organization says about itself, and drafting that starts from it rather than from a blank page. The definitions hold because everyone is drawing from the same place.

---

#### 14. Where the questions go now

A twenty-four-year-old has questions she would once have taken to a mentor: what to do with her life, what the church is for, how to disciple anyone. She asks a model at eleven at night, because it is available and patient. It answers from whatever it absorbed about the church, most of which is not from people who have done the work.

**Without AI.** She asked a person, or she asked nobody.

**With AI.** Whether her question meets thirty years of careful thinking or the average of the internet depends on whether that thinking was made legible to the systems answering her. That is currently being decided by default.

---

#### 15. Formation that turns into information

A course participant finishes a reading at eleven at night. It has opened something uncomfortable. Her cohort meets Thursday. She resolves the discomfort herself, quickly, in the direction that costs least, and by Thursday it is closed. Completion rates for the course look fine.

**Without AI.** Smaller cohorts and more facilitators, available at the hour the reading actually lands. This is expensive and it is the reason formation has never scaled.

**With AI, carefully.** Something inside the pathway that holds the question open instead of closing it, complicates her framing from the author's own material, and points her toward the exercise and toward people. The author reads sample conversations before it is deployed, and real conversations are sampled weekly. If it substitutes for people rather than pushing her toward them, it is withdrawn.

---

#### 16. A body of work that never becomes a course

An author has thirty years of material in print and no course. Building one means sequencing it, writing exercises, and designing what a participant does — eighteen months with an instructional designer who first has to learn the material well enough to structure it. Estimates come in above six figures. He publishes another book instead.

**Without AI.** The cost and the timeline are the reason most serious thinkers publish and stop there.

**With AI.** The corpus becomes searchable, a structure is proposed from his own material, and he writes and approves the content. The assembly compresses. Deciding what actually forms a person does not.

---

#### 17. Peers who cannot see each other's work

Two leaders have been developing related ideas for fifteen years. Each has read one book by the other. Neither can see the shape of what the other has built, because it is spread across publishers, platforms, podcasts, and sites that have shut down.

**Without AI.** Conferences, mutual friends, and chance. The field's actual intellectual structure stays invisible to the people inside it.

**With AI.** Shared vocabulary, shared citation conventions, and links pointing to canonical sources. A hundred separate voices become readable as one field of practice with a hundred named contributors — by readers, and by the systems that decide what gets surfaced.

---

#### 18. The platform that shut down

An author published for eleven years on a platform that no longer exists. Some of that writing is on the Internet Archive, some is in an export file nobody can open, and some is gone. This has happened to most authors who have been publishing for more than fifteen years, usually more than once.

**Without AI.** Someone works through the archive page by page, deciding what is worth keeping and reformatting it by hand. It has no deadline, so it waits, and more becomes unrecoverable each year.

**With AI.** Recovery, conversion, and structuring become cheap enough to finish. Deciding what deserves to come back is still his — some of it should stay gone.

---

#### 19. What happens to the work after him

An author is sixty-eight. His work will outlive him. The only complete account of it is in his head: which arguments he has abandoned, which framework replaced which, where a new reader should start. He has no plan for this and no deadline forcing one.

**Without AI.** A literary executor works from a shelf of books and their own judgment, after he is gone.

**With AI.** The corpus is gathered and structured while he is alive to correct it, and the account of what he holds, what he revised, and what he got wrong is something he approves rather than something reconstructed later.

---

#### 20. Citations that cannot be checked

An author quotes his own book in an article and gives no page number, because the PDF he copied from lost its pagination in conversion. A reader who wants to verify the quote cannot. Across a field, this produces work that cites summaries of sources nobody has opened.

**Without AI.** Check every quotation against the physical book by hand, every time.

**With AI.** Conversion that preserves pagination and footnotes rather than flattening them, plus a validation pass proving nothing was dropped, read by a person. It is the smallest item here and everything else depends on it.

---

#### 21. Voice drift caught by the reader

An author works with an assistant and an editor. Over eight months and a dozen pieces, something in the writing shifts. A long-time reader mentions that the recent work feels different and cannot say how. He rereads and agrees with her.

**Without AI.** Voice is discussed in adjectives, which cannot be checked or argued with, so drift is caught late by readers rather than early by editors. Most readers do not mention it. They read less.

**With AI.** Drafts compared against his own approved baseline on named markers, reporting distance rather than judging quality, and returning *cannot assess* rather than a number when no baseline exists. He decides whether the distance matters; sometimes it is him changing his mind.

---

#### 22. What the saved time is for

An executive director adopts several of the above and saves her team roughly a day a week. Within six months the day is gone, absorbed into more output — more posts, more reports, more email, produced faster and read less. Nobody decided this. Nothing on the dashboard flagged it.

**Without a decision about where the time goes,** every item on this list becomes a productivity story, and the work that was supposed to benefit — being in the room, sitting with the person, going where you were sent — gets crowded out by the volume the tools make possible.

**With one,** the hours are assigned in advance to the things none of this can do. No system enforces that. It is a management decision, made repeatedly.
