### 1 · Why publish at all

Start with the objection, because it is a good one.

You watched a generation of platform-building in the church and most of it was bad. People with a fraction of the substance built large followings and got invited to say things they had not earned the right to say. Conferences became auditions. Books got written to establish a brand rather than because someone had something to say. And some people you knew were slowly deformed by being watched, and did not entirely come back.

You stayed local instead. You did work nobody could see because it was the work in front of you. That cost you money and influence, and in some cases it cost you the ability to shape a conversation you started.

You were right, and this playbook is not an argument that you were wrong.

#### What changed is not you

Your name is already in circulation, and has been for years.

The restatements of your work are out there. Posts by people working from a summary. Conference notes. Somebody's seminary paper. The tidy version of your idea, which travels further than yours because it is easier to repeat and does not contain the hard part.

That was tolerable for most of your career, because a serious person who wanted the real thing could go and find the book.

That is no longer how it works. Increasingly the first move, and often the only move, is that someone asks a machine. And it answers, immediately, fluently, with no hesitation and no source. It answers from whatever it could reach, which is the restatements, because your own words are sitting in page images and untranscribed conference audio and articles on sites that closed years ago.

So the reader receives a competent, confident answer in your name that is not your position. And nothing about it looks wrong, because a wrong answer from a machine is formally identical to a right one.

The choice was never between being visible and being invisible. That choice closed. The choice is between your version and someone else's, and abstaining does not empty the field. It concedes it.

#### Findable, not seen

This is the distinction the whole playbook rests on.

Being seen is something you pursue, and pursuing it is what deformed the people you watched. Being findable is a property of the work. It matters only because somebody is already looking.

And they are. A pastor at eleven at night who has to decide something before Sunday. A young leader who read one paragraph of yours inside somebody else's book and wants to know where it came from. A woman in her second year of a plant who is about to quit and does not know you wrote four hundred pages about exactly what is happening to her.

They are searching right now. The question in front of you is not whether you want an audience. It is whether the person searching reaches your work or a summary of it.

Everything that follows is about that person, not about you.

#### The four motions

A body of work travels through four motions. They happen in order, they each fail differently, and most leaders are strong in one and empty in another without knowing which.

**Meet.** Someone who does not know the work encounters it.

**Hold.** That encounter becomes a relationship you keep rather than a visit you lose.

**Show.** The person is given something they can check.

**Send.** The person carries it into their own context and hands it to somebody else.

Send is the point. The other three exist to make it possible.

### 2 · Meet

*Someone who does not know the work encounters it.*

#### What success actually is

Not awareness. Not reach. These specific events:

A pastor types the exact question she has to answer this week into a search box, and the page that answers it best is yours.

Someone asks an assistant to explain a concept you originated, and the answer comes back with your name and a link to your page, because your words were reachable and the paraphrases were not.

A leader listens to an hour-long conversation with you on a podcast he already subscribes to, and afterward looks you up.

A peer in your field cites you in something they wrote, and three of their readers follow the link.

A denominational leader is handed your name by someone she trusts, searches it, and finds a body of work rather than a speaker bio.

Each of those is a specific person completing a specific action. If you cannot picture the person, the tactic is not working.

#### What serves this motion

| Type | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pillar and cluster articles | 9 | The only asset that answers the question in the form it gets asked |
| Podcast guesting | 9 | Borrows an audience that already exists, at almost no cost |
| Co-bylines with peers | 8 | Borrows a domain the systems already trust |
| Essays | 6 | Found by people looking for a thinker, not an answer |
| Speaking, recorded and posted | 6 | Permanent asset on somebody else's indexed site |
| Academic or journal work | 6 | Slow, and the most durable citation available |
| Books | 5 | High authority, low discoverability on their own |
| Social posts | 5 | Real reach, no permanence |
| Diagnostic tools | 4 | Found by people looking for help rather than ideas |
| Screen or video capture | 2 | Almost invisible to discovery |
| Story page | 2 | Nobody searches for you before they know you |
| Newsletter | 1 | Only reaches people you already have |

#### What unlocks it

**Clusters, not articles.** A single excellent piece is close to invisible. Six to eight interlinked pieces around one idea, each answering a different real question, all pointing to one definitional page, is what search and retrieval systems resolve to. If you can only commit to three pieces, choose a narrower subject rather than writing three orphans.

**Answer the question in the form it gets asked.** "What is covocational ministry and how does a bivocational planter actually structure a week" is findable. "Reflections on vocation in a fragmented age" is not, however good it is.

**Put a liftable answer near the top.** Two or three sentences that fully answer the question and could be quoted whole. That is what a machine extracts, and being quoted is the new front door.

**Say yes to podcasts, and favour the ones that post transcripts.** An untranscribed episode is reach without permanence. Take everything inside your existing network before pitching anyone cold; the conversion difference is enormous.

**A co-byline does something you cannot do alone.** A piece under your name and a peer's, on a site that already has authority, moves you further in one publication than a year of your own posting. It costs a conversation.

#### A worked cluster

*Composite. The shape is what matters.*

A leader known for covocational ministry decides that is his cluster. Not "missional church," which is broader and which fifty other people already own. The narrower one, which he named.

**The hub.** *What covocational ministry actually is.* The definitional page. Answers the plain question completely, in the first three sentences, in a form that could be quoted whole. Everything else in the cluster links here and this links to everything else.

**Seven pieces, each a different real question:**

1. Covocational or bivocational: the distinction and why it matters
2. How a covocational planter actually structures a week
3. What a sending church owes a covocational planter
4. Money: what covocational leaders actually live on
5. The three ways covocational plants fail
6. What covocational ministry asks of a spouse
7. When covocational is the wrong call

*Why these seven.* Each is something a person types when they have a decision in front of them. None is a reflection. Four of them are the questions he has been asked most often in twenty years of training, which is the best available research and costs nothing.

*Why number seven exists.* A cluster containing a piece that argues against its own subject is more credible than one that does not, and it is the piece a skeptical reader opens first.

**What makes it a cluster rather than seven articles:** every piece links to the hub and to at least two siblings, with real sentences rather than a related-posts widget. The hub lists all seven. A reader arriving at piece four can see the shape of the whole thing.

**What to do next.** Nothing. Do not begin a second cluster until this one is complete and has been live for six months. A finished cluster compounds. Two half-clusters do not.

#### Failure modes

Writing the piece you want to write instead of the one being searched for, and then concluding that discovery does not work.

Publishing orphans. Ten unconnected pieces on ten subjects produce almost nothing; six connected pieces on one subject compound.

Chasing platforms instead of questions. Effort spent on social reach that would have been better spent on one cluster.

Titles that are beautiful and unsearchable.

#### One honest measure

Ask three people outside your circle to search for the question you most want to be found for. Watch what they type and what they get. Do that again in six months. That is more informative than any dashboard, and you can run it this week.

### 3 · Hold

*The encounter becomes a relationship you keep.*

#### What success actually is

A woman reads one article, gives you an email address, and eight months later is still opening what you send.

Someone replies to a letter you wrote, disagreeing, and you have an exchange.

A leader forwards your letter to two colleagues without being asked.

A person who found you two years ago finally writes to say they are ready to talk.

The measure here is not list size. It is whether anyone is still there in a year.

#### What serves this motion

| Type | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The letter or newsletter | 10 | The only channel you own outright |
| A diagnostic tool | 9 | The one thing worth an email address, because it is about them |
| The welcome sequence | 8 | Delivers rather than nurtures |
| Field guides and long-form guides | 7 | Substantial enough to be worth an exchange |
| Pathways | 6 | Gives a reason to return on a schedule |
| Your own podcast | 6 | Holds well, costs enormously |
| Courses | 5 | Holds the enrolled, reaches nobody else |

#### What unlocks it

**The letter must be worth opening on its own.** The near-universal failure is the digest: here is what I published this month. That gets skimmed once and unsubscribed. A letter containing a thought that exists nowhere else gets read and answered.

**Give them something about themselves, not about you.** A PDF explaining your framework is about you. A diagnostic that tells a leader where their own church actually stands is about them, and that is the difference between an email address given and an email address withheld.

**Ask for a reply and mean it.** End with a real question. Read the answers. This audience will write back at rates that surprise people who came from commercial publishing, because you are asking them about their work and almost nobody does.

**Reliability beats frequency by a wide margin.** Every six weeks, kept for four years, builds something that weekly-for-three-months does not.

**Four emails, not twelve.** Deliver the thing immediately. Say who you are, briefly. Point at one specific piece of work. Ask a question. Nothing that reads like a sequence.

#### A worked letter

*Composite. Roughly 300 words, which is a normal length.*

> **Subject: What I was wrong about at the elders' meeting**
>
> Something happened on Tuesday that I have not stopped thinking about.
>
> We were three hours into a conversation about whether to plant again, and I realized I had been arguing for something I do not actually believe anymore. I have been saying for about six years that a church should not plant until it has surplus. I have said it in rooms all over [region]. On Tuesday one of our elders asked me to name a church that had ever actually had surplus, and I could not.
>
> I think what I have been calling prudence is closer to a fear of looking irresponsible.
>
> I am not ready to say the opposite. There is a real thing underneath the caution and I have watched plants fail because a sending church could not carry them. But the version I have been teaching is too clean and I have been teaching it confidently, which is the part that bothers me.
>
> That is what the letter is going to be, mostly. Things I am working out, while I am working them out, rather than after.
>
> Every month, roughly. Sometimes there will be a longer piece and I will point you to it, but the letter is not an announcement service.
>
> One question, and I would like an actual answer if you have two minutes. **What is the thing you have been teaching confidently that you are no longer sure about?** I am asking because I suspect most of us have one and almost nobody says it out loud.
>
> [Name]

*What makes it work. A specific Tuesday. An admission that costs something. No links, no announcements, no products. A question the reader will actually want to answer, about their own work rather than about the writer's. And the cadence stated in one clause without ceremony.*

*What the failed version looks like: "Welcome to my newsletter. Here's what you can expect." Nobody replies to that, and the reply is the entire point.*

#### Failure modes

The digest letter.

A lead magnet that is a brochure.

Going quiet for five months and returning with an apology. The apology is worse than the gap.

Building a list and never writing to it, which is the most common failure in this network and the most wasteful.

#### One honest measure

Open rate on the sixth issue compared with the first. Everything else is noise. If the sixth is holding, you have a relationship. If it has collapsed, you have a mailing list.

### 4 · Show

*The person is given something they can check.*

#### What success actually is

A reader clicks a citation and lands on the page it came from, and reads the surrounding paragraph.

Someone who suspected you were repeating a fashionable idea reads the piece where you describe getting it wrong, and revises their opinion of you.

A skeptical denominational executive finds a record of where you have taught and who cited you, and stops wondering whether your reception is real.

A person asks your platform something you never wrote about, and it says it does not have that, and they trust the next answer more.

This is the motion nobody executes well, and it is the one where the work either holds up or does not.

#### What serves this motion

| Type | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| How I learned it | 10 | A specific instance, including the part where you were wrong |
| Books | 10 | The strongest proof artifact that exists |
| Case studies | 9 | Removes the reader's imagination burden |
| Screen or video capture | 9 | Cheapest credibility available; cannot be faked |
| Academic or journal work | 9 | Proof that survives contest |
| The corpus and library | 8 | Volume, checkable, unnarrated |
| Citations and speaking record | 8 | Somebody else attesting, which is worth more than your claim |
| The story page | 8 | Answers why you, once, properly |
| Essays | 7 | Show thinking rather than results |

#### What unlocks it

**Include the part where you were wrong.** This is the single most common way this motion gets ruined, and it gets ruined by the author trying to look competent. A clean account of a thing that went well reads as promotional and is discounted entirely. The account of the plant that failed, the framework you abandoned, the ten years you spent on an idea you now think was half right, is what makes everything else believable.

**One checkable thing per piece.** A citation someone can open. A number with a date. A named place. "It went well" is not evidence and reads as its opposite.

**Let someone else do the attesting.** A record of who has cited you, where you have taught, and who has endorsed what, with names and dates attached, does something your own prose cannot. Build the page. Keep it current.

**Show before you explain.** Put the thing in front of them and let them judge it. The explanation is welcome afterward. Reversed, it reads as a claim awaiting proof.

**Refusal is proof.** A system, or a person, that says plainly when it does not know is more credible on everything else. This is true of your platform and it is true of your writing.

#### A worked example

*Composite, not a real person. The annotations are the point.*

A leader who has trained planters for twenty years wants to write the piece that proves he knows what he is talking about. Here is the version that works, with the reasoning alongside it.

---

**Title: The plant I told them to keep going**

> In 2009 I told a couple in their fourth year to hold on for one more season. They held on for two. The marriage did not survive it, and I have spent fifteen years working out what I got wrong.

*Why this opening. A date, a specific situation, and a real cost inside two sentences. No framing paragraph. A reader knows within four seconds that this is not promotional, and that is the only thing that buys attention for the next eight minutes.*

> I want to be careful about what I am claiming. I do not think I caused it. Two adults made their own decisions and there were things in that marriage I did not know about. But I was the person they called, I was the one with twenty years on them, and what I said was wrong.

*Concede before arguing. This paragraph is what keeps the piece from being either self-flagellation or a dodge. A reader who suspects performed humility relaxes here.*

> What I told them came from a real conviction, which is that most plants that fail do so about eighteen months before they would have turned. I had watched that happen four times. I had the pattern and I was proud of the pattern.
>
> What I did not have was any way of telling the difference between a plant in the hard middle and a plant that was already over. I thought perseverance was the variable. I think now that perseverance is what you apply after you have answered a prior question, and I had no method for the prior question.

*The turn, and it is a change in belief rather than new information. The reader watches him become wrong about something. That is what Show means, and it is why a smooth account proves nothing: there is nothing to watch.*

> Here is what I use now, and I want to say plainly that it came out of that failure rather than out of research.
>
> Three questions, asked of the planter and separately of two people in the core group. Is anyone new coming, at any rate at all. Can the planter's household name one good thing from the last ninety days. Is there anyone besides the planter holding the vision.
>
> If all three are no, the honest counsel is not perseverance. [SPECIFIC: what he says instead.]

*The checkable artifact. Small, usable on Tuesday, and produced by the failure rather than appended to it. A reader now takes something away even if they never make contact.*

> I have used it with [number] planters since [year]. [What happened. A number, a range, or an honest "I have not tracked this well enough to give you a figure," which is itself credible.]

*One checkable result. If the honest answer is that he has not measured it, saying so is stronger than a vague claim, because a reader who has been sold to before is scanning for exactly the vague claim.*

> I still tell people to keep going more often than I tell them to stop. The pattern was not wrong. It was incomplete, and incomplete convictions are the expensive kind, because they are true often enough to keep you confident.

*The close does not resolve into a lesson. It hands the reader a usable idea about their own convictions, which is what makes a piece get forwarded.*

---

**The five things that made it a Show piece:** a dated specific instance, a named cost, a belief that visibly changed, one artifact a reader can use, and one checkable result. Remove any of the five and it reverts to a competent essay that proves nothing.

**What most leaders write instead:** the same material with the failure removed, the framework presented as arrived-at rather than paid-for, and the cost mentioned in a subordinate clause. That version is easier to write, more comfortable to publish, and worth almost nothing.

#### Failure modes

The smooth account. Nobody believes it and everybody has read it.

Credentials without artifacts. A list of degrees and posts proves you were somewhere, not that you know anything.

Claiming expertise. Any sentence containing the word is doing the opposite of its job.

Hiding the failed project because it complicates the narrative. It is the narrative.

#### One honest measure

Could a stranger, in ten minutes, verify three specific claims you make about your own work? If not, this motion is empty regardless of how much you have published.

### 5 · Send

*The person carries it into their own context and hands it on.*

#### What success actually is

A pastor runs your framework with her elders on a Tuesday night, using your language, without you in the room.

Somebody teaches your material to a group of twenty, and one of the twenty teaches it to their own team a year later.

A network leader adopts your tool as standard practice for the planters they train.

A seminary puts your book on a syllabus and forty students a year encounter it.

Someone writes to say a decision went differently because of something you published, and names the decision.

**Send is multiplication, not purchase.** For most people writing playbooks like this, the fourth motion is a transaction. For you it is that somebody else can now do the thing without you. That is what you have spent your life on, and it is the only reason the other three motions are worth the trouble.

Enrollment and purchase happen, and they matter for sustaining the work. They are downstream effects. If they become the definition of Send, the whole framework has gone wrong.

#### What serves this motion

| Type | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The welcome and follow-up sequence | 9 | One clear next step, delivered at the right moment |
| Tools designed to be run with a group | 8 | The reader becomes a facilitator |
| Courses and cohorts | 8 | Formation over time, with peers |
| Pathways | 8 | A sequence someone can walk and then lead |
| Case studies | 7 | Shows what acting on it actually looked like |
| Field guides | 6 | Portable, reusable, made to be handed over |
| Speaking and training | 6 | The oldest form of this, and still effective |

#### What unlocks it

**Build for the second reader, not the first.** The question is not whether your reader is moved. It is whether they can hand it to somebody else. A tool with a facilitator's page, a framework with a one-page summary, a pathway someone can lead without having taken it: those are the artifacts that multiply.

**One ask per piece.** A piece pointing at three things points at nothing. The same single next step every time, and it should be small.

**Make the next step something they do, not something they buy.** Run this with your team. Ask your staff these three questions. Try it once and see what happens. Purchase can come later, and it converts better after someone has already acted.

**Name what happened when someone else did it.** A reader deciding whether to run something with their elders wants to know how it went for a peer. That is what a case study is for.

#### A worked Send artifact

Take the three questions from the Show example above. As a paragraph inside an article they are an idea. Here is what turns them into something that multiplies.

**The thing itself, on one page.** The three questions, who to ask them of, and what the answers mean. Printable. No login.

**A facilitator's page.** How to run it with a group in forty minutes. What to do when the planter and the core group answer differently, which is the most common and most important outcome. Two sentences on what not to say.

**The honest limits.** What this does not tell you. When to ignore it. A tool that names its own boundaries gets used by careful people, which is who you want using it.

**One account of it going badly.** A time the answers were all yes and the plant closed anyway, and what that turned out to mean.

**Nothing that requires you.** No booking link, no "contact me to discuss your results." If the artifact needs you present, it does not multiply, and multiplication is the whole motion.

**What this costs:** an afternoon. What it does that the article cannot: a reader who runs it with their elders on Tuesday has now taught your framework to eight people, and one of them will run it somewhere else.

#### Failure modes

Ending pieces with "reach out if you would like to talk."

Building the course before you know what people actually need, which is a year of work aimed at a guess.

Treating Send as the sales motion. This audience will notice within a paragraph.

Making the material dependent on your presence, which feels like protecting quality and is the thing that stops multiplication.

#### One honest measure

How many people have taught your material without you being involved? Ask. You will be surprised in both directions, and you cannot know without asking.

### 6 · The diagnostic

The most useful page in this book.

Score yourself honestly on each motion, out of ten, using the observable events from each chapter rather than your impression.

**Meet.** Can a stranger with your exact question find you? Search it yourself, from a browser you are not logged into.

**Hold.** How many people have chosen to hear from you again, and are they still opening?

**Show.** Could a skeptic verify three of your claims in ten minutes?

**Send.** Who has taught your material without you?

Then find the lowest number and build there.

That instruction sounds obvious and is almost never followed, because building where you are already strong is enjoyable and feels productive. A leader with thirty years of proof will keep producing proof. A leader with a large list will keep writing to it. Neither changes anything.

Two patterns account for most leaders in this network.

**Full Show, empty Meet.** Six books, decades of teaching, an unassailable record, and invisible to anyone who does not already know the name. The work is proven and unreachable. Everything should go into clusters, citations, and peer links, and nothing into further proof.

**Full Hold, empty Send.** A real relationship with a real group of people, and no artifact any of them could use without you. The work is loved and unmultipliable. Everything should go into tools, pathways, and facilitator materials.

### 7 · Two starting positions

The framework is the same. The sequences are opposite.

#### A large corpus, and no findability

You have books, decades of talks, articles on sites you do not control, and training material built for particular rooms and never gathered.

**Show is already full.** Resist adding to it. Another book will not fix this and will cost you two years.

**Start with gathering.** Until the corpus is reachable, nothing else works. Books converted properly, talks transcribed, articles recovered, all of it in one place that can be searched and cited. This is unglamorous and it is the whole foundation.

**Then one cluster, completed.** Choose the idea that is most yours, ideally one you named. Six to eight pieces, interlinked, each answering a question people actually ask. Do not start a second cluster until the first is finished.

**Then peer links.** You have thirty years of relationships and they are the fastest asset available to you. Three co-bylines or substantive mutual citations will do more than a year of solo publishing.

**Hold runs alongside from day one,** because the traffic that arrives before you have a letter is lost permanently.

**Send comes last, and it comes out of the cluster.** By then you will know which two pieces people actually use, and those become the tool or the pathway.

Approximate order: gather, letter open, one cluster, peer links, then tools.

#### Real substance, nothing published

You have twenty years of practice, strong convictions, and no body of published work.

**Show is your empty column, and it gates everything else.** A stranger who finds you and is not convinced does not subscribe, and a subscriber who is not convinced does not act. So proof comes first even though it feels backwards.

**Start with three how-I-learned-it pieces.** Specific instances from your own practice, each including the part where you were wrong, each with one checkable detail. These are the only pieces you can write that nobody else could.

**Then the story page.** Why you, answered once, properly.

**Then Hold,** which is cheap: the letter and one diagnostic tool.

**Then Meet, which is the slow one.** A cluster takes months to compound and there is no way to accelerate it. Podcast guesting runs in parallel from the first week, because it borrows rather than builds.

**Send is last and small.** One thing a reader can run with their own team.

Approximate order: three proof pieces, story, letter, cluster, guesting throughout, then one tool.

### 8 · A rule of life for published work

Rhythm is where almost everyone fails, and the failure is always the same shape: an intense start, four months, silence, and then a returning apology.

The apology is worse than the gap. It teaches your readers that you are unreliable and that they were right not to invest.

#### Choose a rhythm you will not resent in eight months

Not the rhythm you can sustain in your most energetic month. The one you can keep in your worst quarter, when someone in your congregation dies and a funder pulls out and your daughter is ill.

For most leaders in this network that is monthly. For several it is quarterly. Quarterly, kept for ten years, is forty substantial pieces and an unbroken relationship with everyone who has stayed. That is a considerable body of work and it is available to anyone.

Weekly is not better. Weekly is how people burn out and then blame the medium.

#### The rule, stated

**One long piece per cycle.** Whatever your cycle is.

**One letter per cycle, minimum.** Short is fine. Written the week it goes out, not batched three months ahead, because batched letters read like batched letters.

**One yes per cycle to somebody else's platform.** A podcast, a co-byline, a guest piece. This is the cheapest growth available and it requires no writing.

**One fallow period per year, announced.** Say you are going quiet in August and then go quiet. Announced silence costs nothing. Unannounced silence costs the relationship.

That is four commitments. At quarterly, that is sixteen obligations a year, and it is genuinely sustainable alongside a full life.

#### What to do when you miss

Say so plainly in the next one, in one sentence, and continue. Do not apologize at length, do not explain, do not promise a new schedule. The people still reading do not need the account and the ones who left will not see it.

### 9 · Corroboration

This chapter is the one no individual can act on alone, and the one that only this network can do.

#### What has always been true

Credibility in your world has never moved through platforms. It has moved through vouching.

A senior practitioner introduces a peer to a denominational team and says they have stood next to each other in this work for twenty years. That introduction transfers something no résumé transfers. Everyone reading this got the invitations that mattered through some version of it.

#### What is now also true

Until recently, the internet could not see any of that. It saw individual pages and had no way of registering that two people had been corroborating each other for two decades.

It can now. Not through a trick. The systems that mediate discovery evaluate networks rather than single pages, and a public, reciprocal, substantive link between two people who have actually stood next to each other reads as exactly what it is.

Which means the oldest practice in your world now has a digital form, and it is the one move a solo platform cannot fake, because it requires the other person to exist and to have agreed.

#### What a real citation looks like

Not a link exchange. Not a logo strip. Not a list of friends.

**You engage with what they actually wrote.** Quote a specific claim, agree or disagree with it substantively, and link to the piece where they made it. The way you would in a book.

**It is reciprocal but not transactional.** They cite you when your work is relevant to theirs, not because you cited them last month.

**It survives a reader's scrutiny.** Someone following the link should find that the citation was accurate and that the engagement was real.

**Disagreement counts, and counts double.** A public, respectful disagreement between two people who have worked alongside each other for twenty years is more credible than agreement, and it is nearly absent from this field.

#### What to do

In your next long piece, engage with one thing a peer in this network has written. Really engage. Then tell them you did.

Do that every cycle and within two years the network is visibly what it has always actually been.

### 10 · Authorship

Everything published under your name is yours. That is the standard, and this chapter is how it is kept.

#### What must be yours

**The thinking.** Always, without exception.

**The argument.** The shape of the case, what it concedes, where it lands.

**The voice.** How you say it, including the words you would never use.

**Ratification.** Every sentence read and approved by you before it goes out under your name. Not skimmed. Read.

#### Where a machine may work

**Gathering.** Finding what you have already written on a subject, across everything you have made. This is the largest gain available and it changes nothing about authorship.

**Structuring.** Proposing an order for material you have supplied.

**Drafting from your own corpus,** where every claim is traceable to something you actually wrote, and where you then rewrite it.

**Checking.** Finding your unsupported claims, your repetitions, the place where you have contradicted something you wrote in 2014.

#### Where it may not

Anything a person told you in confidence. Anything pastoral. Anything where the reader believes a person attended to them. Anything about a minor. Anything you have not read.

#### Provenance, in practice

Say once, somewhere findable, how your work is made. Something like: *I write everything published here. AI helps me search my own corpus and find things I have written elsewhere. Sources are named. Nothing is invented.*

That sentence costs nothing when it is true and is unrecoverable when it is discovered to be false.

#### The test

Would you be comfortable if the process were described publicly, in detail, by someone unsympathetic? If yes, proceed. If you would want it quiet, do not do it.

---

### 11 · Refusals

#### What not to publish

Anything said to you in confidence, in any disguised form.

Anything identifying a person in your care, particularly a minor, and particularly not with a photograph.

A settled version of an idea you have not settled. If you are mid-thought on the thing you are best known for, wait.

Anything you would not say in the room to the person it concerns.

Anything written to establish something rather than because you have something to say. You will know. That is the deformation you spent thirty years avoiding.

#### What not to hand to a machine

Pastoral material of any kind.

Anything whose value is that you sat with it. Some of what you write is worth reading precisely because it cost you three weeks, and a version produced in four minutes is not a faster version of it.

Anything you will not read line by line before it goes out.

#### Who should decline all of this

**If publishing would change what you write.** Stop. It is not worth a platform, and the network is better off without a leader who was talked into it.

**If you cannot sustain any rhythm at all,** even quarterly. A platform that goes silent is worse than none.

**If the work genuinely is not finished.**

**If your instinct says no for a reason you cannot yet articulate.** The people in this network have unusually good instincts about this. Trust yours over any argument in this book.

A faithful no is a real option. This chapter exists so that it is a real one.

### 13 · The first ninety days

One page.

**Weeks one and two.** Run the diagnostic. Score all four motions honestly using the observable events, not your impression. Find the lowest. Write down which motion you are building and do not change it for ninety days.

**Weeks three to six.** Open the letter, whatever else you do. Write the first one and send it to whoever you have, even if that is forty people. Choose your rhythm and write it down.

**Weeks seven to ten.** Publish one substantial piece serving your emptiest motion. Not a launch. One piece, finished properly, on your own platform.

**In it, engage with one peer's work.** Substantively. Then tell them.

**Weeks eleven and twelve.** Send the second letter. Say yes to one podcast or guest slot. Look at what happened, and change one thing.

That is the whole first quarter. One piece, one citation, one rhythm, one yes.

It is smaller than you expect and larger than almost anyone actually does.
