### Content Strategy for Movement Leaders
##### The Movemental single source of truth for how to think about your public work

*For the movement leaders of the hundred — and for the team who serves them. This is the canonical account of why we make content the way we do, what to make, and how. It is written to you, the leader, in the voice we mean to keep: partners who have been on this road, not experts standing over you.*

---

#### A word before the strategy

You did not say yes to becoming a content creator. You said yes to letting your life-work — the books, the talks, the frameworks, the fragments scattered across fifteen years of platforms that may or may not still exist — be gathered into a coherent home that the age can recognize. Hold onto that. Everything in this document serves that yes. The moment any of it starts to feel like feeding a machine, we have lost the plot, and you should tell us.

So read this less as a marketing playbook and more as an account of stewardship. The credibility was already yours. We are making sure it survives.

---

#### Part I — Why this exists

Strategy that doesn't start with the real problem becomes technique for its own sake. So start with the problem, which has a name: **fragmentation.**

Take one real person. You have spent decades planting, teaching, writing, shaping how people understand the church on mission. The work is real and it is vast. But ask the simple question — *where is it?* The honest answer is a list of scattered containers. Books locked in formats that don't speak to each other. Articles on old sites. Talks surviving as unindexed video, or not at all. Frameworks you originated that now live more in other people's paraphrases than in any home of your own. **The work was whole in you. It has been scattered into the world, and most people will never do the dig.**

Fragmentation has two faces, and you feel both:

- **Informational fragmentation — what you know, scattered.** Your ideas exist, but in pieces, across containers that can't see each other. No center of gravity. When someone searches the very question you have spent your life answering, they don't find you; they find noise, or someone else's version of your idea.
- **Relational fragmentation — who you know, scattered, and invisible.** Because your work is in pieces, you cannot see the people moving through it. You don't know that a reader found one idea in a post, bought a book, took a course, and started teaching it in her own church. **That journey — which is the entire point — is invisible to you, because the work the journey runs through is itself shattered.** One scattering, two faces: what is known and who it is for, both broken in the same event.

Now bring AI into that room. People assume it will pull the pieces together. By default it does the opposite. AI is a mirror; it reflects back what it is given, and what it is given is fragmentation. Ask a model to represent a scattered author and it assembles an answer from whatever shards it can find and hands back something that wears the name without carrying the person. That is what everyone has learned to call slop. **Slop is not simply low quality. Slop is fragmentation, reflected and multiplied** — and it flows back into the world, gets indexed, gets trained on, and becomes the next layer of the scattering.

Underneath even that sits the **authorship break**: for the first time in five hundred years, a faithful reader opening your newest piece has to ask a question that never had to be asked — *is this still you, or a model wearing your name?* When people can't answer and can't keep paying the cost of wondering, they withdraw. And publication — the word that crosses distance and outlives the one who wrote it — is one of the oldest ways truth has traveled. This is not a marketing problem. It is closer to a civilizational one.

**That is why content strategy exists here.** Not to grow a following. To heal fragmentation — to gather what is scattered into a coherent home, to make the invisible journey visible, and to keep your authorship legible in an age that would otherwise dissolve it.

#### Part II — The one conviction underneath everything

Before any tactic, one conviction: **the word is yours, and it comes first.**

You have something to say that is genuinely yours — a body of thinking, hard-won and lived. That authored word is generative and prior. It is the substrate from which everything else is made. Strategy *serves* it; strategy never originates it. We will use AI as the infrastructure around the writing — to gather, to shape into findable form, to surface the journey. We will not use AI to author it. Every word that goes out under your name is yours. That distinction is not negotiable, for us or for you.

Two disciplines follow directly, and they are load-bearing for everything below:

- **Corpus-grounding.** Your published work is not merely source material to cite; it is the ground from which new pieces are drawn. Claims, frameworks, and examples come from your actual corpus, paraphrased for the web, never invented. If the corpus doesn't support it, it stays out.
- **No invented quotes.** Every quotation attributed to you is a real quote from a real source, with the citation visible. AI may summarize your positions; it may never put words in your mouth.

Keep these in view. They are what make it *your* platform rather than a machine wearing your name.

---

#### Part III — The frame: SAY → FIND → MOVE

Here is the whole strategy in three moves, in order. This is not a new vocabulary bolted onto your work; it *names and sequences* what your corpus already teaches.

**SAY — the authored word.** What is actually yours to say. This is the moment of authorship (Part II). It is first and non-negotiable. Nothing downstream is worth anything if the word underneath it isn't real and yours.

**FIND — can people meet you where they are searching for what you say, even if they don't know your name?** This is the work of translating your sealed corpus into forms discovery systems can read — and it is exactly the job of the Evergreen Engine (Part VII). Findability does not invent authority; it *renders* authority you already have into a shape machines can see.

**MOVE — can it move them *through* your work?** In two senses at once: as **compelling form** in the medium you're working in, and as **participation in their own transformation** — the way a piece leads someone to grow, not just to know. This is the work of the four necessities and the formation pathways (Part VI).

##### Why the order is the whole game

The order is not cosmetic. "Meet them where they're searching" is a knife that cuts both ways. Run it **first** and it quietly inverts: you start writing toward search demand, chasing what's ranking, until the voice is gone and you are a content farm with good theology — you become the slop. Run it **second**, as a *check on a word you already had to say*, and it becomes faithful distribution instead of pandering.

So: **you say your thing first. Then you ask whether people can find it. Then you ask whether it moves them.** Findability serves authorship. It never precedes it.

##### The two questions

Everything above resolves into two questions you ask *after* you've said what you have to say — and you ask them *with* us, not because we've answered them over your head:

1. **Can people meet you where they are searching for what you say — even if they don't know your name?** (Including your past work, repurposed.)
2. **Can it move them through your work** — both as compelling form, and as their own transformation, the way your work leads them to grow or participates in that growth?

Hold these two questions. They are the diagnostic you run on any single piece and on your body of work as a whole.

#### Part IV — Who it's for and where they are

Relational fragmentation is the invisible journey. The cure is to make that journey visible — which means knowing that your audience is not a single undifferentiated "following" but people at different points on a path. Roughly:

- **Stranger.** Doesn't know your name. Has a question you happen to answer. Finds you by searching — or increasingly, by asking a machine. (Heavily a **FIND** stage.)
- **Knows you.** Has met your work once and is deciding whether to come back. Wants a reason to stay in relationship. (The threshold stage — where an owned channel earns trust.)
- **Being formed.** Has committed to being changed, not just informed. Willing to do the work over time. (Heavily a **MOVE** stage.)
- **Participant / multiplier.** No longer just receiving — practicing, teaching it to others, reproducing the movement. The whole point.

The strategic question that used to feel impossible — *when do I make each kind of thing?* — mostly resolves here. You are not choosing between parallel options. You are choosing **which move you are making, for whom, on this path.** That is answerable in a sentence, and Part V gives you the map.

#### Part V — What to make: the content types as stations on the journey

The seven content types in your studio are not a flat menu. They are **stations along the path from stranger to participant,** each tuned differently across FIND and MOVE. This is the "when and why" — best practices live with the doing (Part VI), not here.

| Type | Leans | Reach for it when… | On the journey |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Article** | FIND | you have a discrete answer to a real question people ask | a stranger finds you |
| **Article (academic)** | FIND + authority | a claim needs evidence, or you want to be *citable* by scholars and by AI engines | a stranger, and the machines, find you credibly |
| **Book** | SAY (deep) | you have a whole framework, not a point — the canonical statement and the wellspring everything else is drawn from | authority + source |
| **Lesson → Course** | MOVE | you want to take someone A→B over time — developmental, not informational | being formed |
| **Newsletter** | MOVE (cadence) | you want to keep a warm, algorithm-proof relationship and a regular presence | knows-you |
| **Podcast** | FIND + MOVE | you think aloud well, or want to leverage guests' networks | discovery + intimacy |
| **Video** | FIND + MOVE | the message needs face, demonstration, or emotion | discovery + compellingness |
| **Event** | MOVE (embodied) | transformation needs bodies and real time — you're catalyzing a moment | audience becomes participant |

A note on the **book**: it sits across the whole thing. It is both the deep authority a stranger might discover *and* the wellspring every other piece repurposes from. In the studio it is quite literally the corpus your platform and your agent draw on. Treat it as the center, not one station among equals.

#### Part VI — How to make it *move*: the four necessities

Here is what separates a **formation engine** from a publishing site, and it is non-negotiable: **transformation over information.** A piece that merely informs — however true, however well-written — has not yet earned the attention it asks for. Across every artifact type, aim to carry the four necessities. A single piece need not fully deliver all four, but it should gesture toward all four, and never be missing all of them.

1. **Dissonance.** The reader meets something that disturbs their existing categories — not for shock, but for the theological and cognitive tension that opens the possibility of seeing differently. A piece that only confirms what the reader already thinks has failed, even if it's true.
2. **Action.** The reader is given something specific and time-bounded to *do* — not "ten things to consider," an actual embodied practice. Action is not optional; it is constitutive of formation.
3. **Reflection.** The reader has a structured way to think about what happened when they acted — the discipline that converts experience into wisdom.
4. **Community.** The reader is pointed toward, or placed in, others doing the same work. Solitary formation is incomplete formation. The platform gathers practitioners, not just individuals.

This is the editorial spine of everything — article, newsletter, course week, event. Its fullest expression is the guided lesson scaffold (Dissonance → Reflection → Practice → Community → Integration → Going Deeper), but the four necessities live in a good article too. When you ask the second question — *does this move them through my work?* — this is what you're checking for.

MOVE's other sense, **compelling form**, is craft: does the piece hold attention and carry feeling in its medium? A dull true thing doesn't move anyone. Both senses matter, and the studio's media surfaces (podcast, video) exist partly so the form can carry what prose alone can't.

#### Part VII — How to make it *found*: the Evergreen Engine

The Engine does one thing: **it takes a finished body of work and makes it findable.** You have already done the hard part — the ideas, the frameworks, the research. What's missing is *form*. A book is a sealed container; search engines can't read it and answer engines can't quote it. The Engine translates that sealed work into the shapes discovery systems are built to read — a definition, a glossary entry, an FAQ answer, a numbered framework. **It does not invent authority. It renders authority you already have into a form the machines can see.**

Three ideas make this real:

- **E-E-A-T** — Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness — is the standard search and answer engines use to judge quality. Most sites strain to fake it. You already have it; it's just trapped in books. Grounding every sentence in your corpus, naming the sources, and holding your vocabulary steady is how real expertise becomes visible to a system that otherwise can't see it.
- **Hub-and-spoke (pillar / cluster).** Your major themes become **pathways** — pillar hubs — and the articles beneath each become the spokes that link inward and outward. Coherence, not just volume, is what makes a body of work legible to both readers and machines. (For Alan, the pathways are mDNA, Metanoia, Reframation, Discipleship, and Movement Intelligence; yours are the major streams of your own work.)
- **Repurposing as multiplication.** This is the same word, stewarded into many shapes: a **book chapter → an article → a lesson → a podcast talking-point → clips → a newsletter issue.** A podcast's **transcript → an article.** An event's **recording → a video, its recap → a newsletter.** Repurposing is not repackaging for its own sake; it is refusing to let a word you already paid for stay sealed in one container.

Findability, done this way, is the answer to Question 1 — and it is how your coherent signal eventually makes its way back into the AI systems themselves, so that when the next generation asks the machine *what is the missional church, what is discipleship, what is movement,* the answer is informed by you, in your own words, rather than by the noise.

#### Part VIII — The Studio as instrument

The frame is not abstract; it is wired into the tool you use. Learning to see the studio through SAY → FIND → MOVE turns a set of fields into a strategy you can feel.

**The DETAILS drawer splits cleanly along your two questions.**

- **The FIND apparatus** — definition anchor, citable statistic, FAQ, citation, and the academic fields — is what makes you answerable to search and to AI engines, even when the reader doesn't know your name. These render your E-E-A-T machine-legible. Filling them well is how a stranger finds you.
- **The MOVE apparatus** — the body's compellingness, plus the pathway / series / *isPartOf* wiring — is what carries someone deeper. The pathway and series fields are not metadata housekeeping; they are how you connect a single piece into the *body* of your work, so a reader can move through it rather than land on an island. This is where you heal relational fragmentation: you make the journey visible.

**The academic modifier** is your citability setting — turn it on when a claim needs evidence scaffolding or when you want to be quoted by scholars and by answer engines. It is FIND in its most authoritative form.

**The publication themes** (mDNA, Metanoia, Reframation) let a piece wear the identity of the stream it belongs to.

So the two questions become a habit at the station: after you've said your thing, glance at the FIND fields (*can they find this?*) and the MOVE wiring (*does this connect into my body of work and carry them somewhere?*). The studio is built to let you answer both.

#### Part IX — Where to begin

Strategy that can't be started is just a poster. So, concretely:

- **Publish first.** The floor is always: get one true thing out, cleanly, to completion. Never let strategy talk block a leader from finishing a piece. Confidence comes from having crossed the line, once.
- **Prove one cluster before scaling.** Take your richest, most foundational theme first and build it out fully — the pillar and its articles, interlinked. That one complete cluster is both the proving ground and the cleanest demonstration of the whole. Then fill the matrix theme by theme; it becomes a fill-the-slots exercise rather than a blank page.
- **Start where fear is lowest and volume is highest.** For most leaders that's **articles and the newsletter** — the FIND floor and the owned relationship. Build toward **course and event**, where the deepest transformation lives but the craft demand is highest.
- **Cadence over heroics.** A regular, owned rhythm (the newsletter) beats sporadic brilliance. Presence is a form of faithfulness.

#### Part X — The non-negotiables

These are the guardrails. When a decision is unclear, these decide it.

- **Every word that goes out under your name is yours.** AI is the infrastructure around the writing, never the writing.
- **Corpus-grounded, sources named, no invented quotes.** If your work doesn't support it, it stays out.
- **Transformation over information.** Every piece gestures toward the four necessities. Informing is not the goal; formation is.
- **Say first — never open a piece from search demand.** Findability is a check on an authored word, not its origin. The day we reverse that, we've started making slop.
- **Partner posture.** We teach and decide *with* you, framing choices and their tradeoffs, not prescribing over you. The strategic calls that are yours to make stay yours.
- **You are not alone in the frame.** Your pieces link to your peers' — the hundred is a *scenius*, a scene whose collective intelligence no one can manufacture, only host. Your coherence deepens theirs, and theirs yours.

#### Coda — what this is finally about

We do not call this a strategy. We call it a **stewardship.**

The wound was fragmentation — your whole word scattered into pieces the world can't reassemble, and the people moving through your work invisible to you. The cure is not more content. It is coherence: your authored word, gathered into a home, made findable in forms the age can read, and wired so that the journey through it becomes visible at last. SAY, then FIND, then MOVE. The credibility was always yours. We are only making sure it survives — and that when someone finds it, it can still move them.
