# Everything Your Platform Can Do: Creator Studio Guide

`MovementalCreatorStudioGuide.pdf` · 2,996 words

Everything Your Platform Can Do. A plain guide to what's waiting in your Creator Studio the day it launches — everything you can write, publish, teach, sell, and tend, all in one place.

---

One place for the whole of your work. When your studio launches, here is what's waiting for you: a single place to write, publish, teach, sell, and stay close to the people your work reaches. Your studio is built around two kinds of work. One is your own making — writing, recording, teaching, publishing. The other is everything that connects you to the people who read, watch, learn, and give: your inbox, your subscribers, your contacts, your numbers, and the wider movement you belong to. Your home screen keeps both within reach. Open it and you can jump straight to writing something new, pick up the piece you had open last, answer the people who have written to you, or look at how your work is doing. It also shows the current Kairos cycle — the movement's shared rhythm — and when it closes, with a way straight in. Nothing here asks you to save. Every word you type and every field you change is kept as you go. This guide walks through both halves in plain terms. Take it as a map of what you can do the moment you sign in.

CON TEN T · WRITE

A calm place to write. The heart of the studio is a clean editor that gets out of your way and keeps everything you have ever written within reach. The editor is quiet by design. When you stop typing, the menus and buttons around the edges fade back, so it is just you and the page. You can rename a piece right where its title sits, move between your drafts, and trust that all of it is being saved. From this one editor you can start seven kinds of work: Articles — long-form writing with images, quotes, citations, and embedded media. Books — many chapters, each its own section you can write and reorder. Lessons — teaching content that can carry quizzes and its own section-by-section navigation. Newsletters — email editions with a subject line, preview text, and delivery settings. Podcasts — audio episodes gathered into a series. Videos — projects built from separate recorded or designed pieces. Events — gatherings with scheduling, registration, and tickets. When you are writing a book, a chapter strip along the top lets you move between chapters, add new ones, reorder them, and switch between the writing, an outline of the whole thing, and the numbers for that book. The outline is its own view — a map of your piece as a set of parts you can rearrange before or while you draft. There is a scholarly mode for academic work. Turn it on and you get structured abstracts, a bibliography, peer-review notes, and proper handling of math and code. Academic pieces can be registered with a DOI when you publish. Alongside the page, a details drawer holds everything else about a piece: its title and description for search, its web address, tags, featured image, who is listed as author, and — for events — the when, where, speakers, agenda, and registration questions. Every saved version is kept, too, so you can look at an earlier draft and bring it back with a single click.

AI help, when you want it. Four assistants sit inside the editor: a writing partner for drafting, refining, and thinking out loud; a voice coach for tone, rhythm, and readability; an SEO guide for titles and keywords; and a guardrails check that keeps you true to your own style guide. As you type, the studio can suggest the next few words — accept them with the Tab key. You can also hand it a passage to rewrite, or open a longer back-and-forth in a side panel.

CON TEN T · RECORD & FILM

Podcasts and video, built in pieces. Audio and video are not one long recording you have to get right in a single take. You build them from separate pieces and assemble them. Each piece can be a talking-head recording, a screen capture, a title card, a whiteboard sketch, an interview setup, a closing credits roll, or a file you upload. You record straight from your camera or microphone in the browser; turn the video off and you get audio only. Some pieces you do not record at all — you design them. Title cards and slide sequences are built with type, layout, and theme controls. And when you are reading from a script, a teleprompter panel scrolls your lines while you record. For video, a timeline workspace lets you arrange the pieces, preview the whole thing, and get it ready to publish. For podcasts, a series view holds your episodes, your show notes, and the audio pulled from your files.

CON TEN T · TEACH

Lessons and courses. A lesson is written much like an article, but it knows it belongs to a course. While you write a lesson, a bar shows which course and which slot it fills, and lets you move it, reassign it, or set it loose. If a lesson does not have a home yet, you can create a course for it right there. Longer lessons show a side navigation so you — and your readers — can jump between sections. You can attach a quiz to any lesson, adding questions and answer choices and editing them in place.

Building a course. A short setup walks you through a new course: its title and description, whether it is a group that moves together or something people take at their own pace, whether it is scheduled or openended, and how it is structured. From there you manage the whole curriculum: rename it, add and reorder and remove lesson slots, drop existing lessons into slots or create a blank lesson for every empty one, set the price, and publish the course as a single thing. For a group course, sessions show up on your calendar, and you can host a discussion thread tied to each session and reply to the people in your cohort.

O RGAN IZE

Everything you've made, in one place. One catalog holds all of it — every article, book, lesson, newsletter, event, podcast, and video, plus the audio clips, images, and files you have uploaded. You can filter by kind or by whether something is a draft or published, search by title, sort by date or length or who is allowed to see it, and switch between a grid of cards and a plain list. Select several pieces at once to archive them, duplicate them, add them to a collection, or handle them together. Collections let you group related pieces under a theme you name. You can create them, rename them, drag pieces in and out, and filter your whole library down to a single one. There is also a four-way tagging system, so you can label a piece by its topic, its format, its audience, and its voice — and find things the way you actually think about them, not just by what kind of thing they are. When you pick a book, a course, or an event, a pricing panel appears on the side: free, a one-time purchase, included with membership, or a mix; ticket types with prices and limits; promo codes. And a calendar — by month or week — shows everything that is scheduled: event dates, cohort sessions, pieces set to publish, and email sends. Click a day to see what is on it, open something, add an event, or drag to move it.

PUBLISH

Publishing, in one guided pass. Publishing is not a separate place you go. It opens as a guided step right from where you are writing or organizing, and walks you through four choices.

Your title, web address, description, and keywords, with tags and a preview of the structured data search engines read. The studio can suggest all of these for you. W HE RE IT GO E S.

Send a piece to your own website, your RSS feed, a PDF shelf, your email subscribers, YouTube, Spotify, Medium, Substack, Amazon KDP, a course export for other learning systems, scheduled social posts, and DOI registration. Whatever you have connected shows as ready; the rest offer to connect.

Choose the visual theme readers see, and set who is allowed in — everyone, free subscribers, paying subscribers, or your founding patrons. Before an email goes out, you see exactly how the subscriber's notification will look, including how a gated piece splits for people who have not paid. Events and courses have their own touches. For an event you can open or close registration, set reminder emails to go out a week and a day before, and share it to Facebook or LinkedIn. A course publishes with all its lessons, pricing, and destinations in one pass. After you publish, the studio does the sending to each place you chose and records what came back — the YouTube video's ID, the DOI number, and so on — for you to check later.

CO MMUN ITY · EN GAGE

Staying close to the people you reach. The other half of the studio is about connection — the conversations around your work and the people having them. THIN K IN G O UT LO UD.

Private threads with the writing AI, where you work a piece over before anyone sees it. Pin them, rename them, tie them to a manuscript, and choose whether you want quiet reflection, honest critique, or help expanding an idea.

Three streams of incoming messages land together: notes from the contact form on your public site — turn a sender into a saved contact with one click; feedback from collaborators, which you can pin, archive, and mark resolved; and comments on your published work, which you can approve, hide, or moderate.

Compose a one-off email to your subscribers: pick who gets it, write it, choose a look, drop in content from something you have already written, send a test to yourself, and send now or schedule it for later.

Turn on automatic emails that go out when someone new subscribes, upgrades to paid, or drifts away — and preview the effect before you switch one on.

For an event, filter the people registered by where they stand or what ticket they hold, write a message that fills in their name and the event details automatically, and send. And paying subscribers who reach out get a familiar chat thread, where you can pin, mark read, and reply.

How your work is doing. The numbers you would want, without having to go dig for them. At the top: your subscriber count, how many you have gained, how much you have published, and your recurring revenue. For published pieces: total views, how far they have reached across every place you sent them, where they rank in search, how engagement rises and falls over time, and a ranked list of your strongest pieces with a quick way to edit or publish again. For work in progress: how many words you have written, the hours you have spent, how closely you are holding to your own style, whether you are keeping pace with your weekly goal, and how your readability is trending. There is more for the parts that need it — where subscribers come from and why some leave, how your emails perform, how many people finish a course and where they drop off, how an event's registration and attendance and revenue came in, and what readers are searching for on your site. You can slice all of it by a single piece, a collection, or a stretch of time — the last week, month, quarter, or all of it. And the same view sits right inside a book while you are editing it.

COMMUN ITY · KAIROS

The movement you belong to. Kairos is the part of the studio that connects you to other leaders, in a shared rhythm of cycles. Each cycle, you get a curated feed of leader-to-leader matches chosen for you — with a sense of why each one fits and a way through to their work. You can record a short check-in, or speak it and have it transcribed, tag up to four things you are working on, and send it into the current cycle. You can also route a request to the network — an introduction, a co-writing partner, an endorsement, a podcast invitation — and tie it to one of your own pieces. And a living log keeps track of the relationships you have built: who introduced whom, who worked together, who carried whose work. If you are the one stewarding a cycle, you review the matches, approve the pairings, and publish the feed for everyone.

THRO UGHO UT

A few things that are always true. Some of what makes the studio feel like the studio is not in any one place — it is everywhere. It saves itself. There is no save button. Every keystroke and change is kept, and a small indicator shows it syncing. One shortcut reaches everything. Press ⌘K anywhere to open the search-and-do bar. It respects your role. If you cannot publish, issue refunds, or manage roles, those buttons simply are not there. The studio shows you what is yours to do. The writing stays quiet. In the editor, the surrounding menus fade when you are not using them. You can run more than one organization and switch between them from the top of the screen. Your published work lands on a real public site — themed pages for every kind of work, with an RSS feed and the plumbing search engines need, served from the same place you write. A setup checklist walks you through going live — your profile, your first publish, uploading files, connecting services, and bringing in your subscribers.
