### The Four-Stage Path — Underlying Logic

*Internal reference. Captures the load-bearing claims that justify why Safety, Sandbox, Skills, and Solutions exist in this specific order — and why the order is not arbitrary. Companion to the three-minute narratives, which are the spoken-aloud expression of the logic captured here. This document is the substrate; the narratives are the surface.*

---

#### The asymmetry that justifies everything

For the organizations Movemental serves — churches, faith-based nonprofits, theological institutions — **credibility is not a competitive advantage. It is the product.** Erode the credibility and the work itself collapses. There is no second product to fall back on.

A marketing agency with an AI mishap loses a client. A church with an AI mishap loses the authority of its pulpit. A nonprofit with an AI mishap loses donor trust, which is the lifeline. A seminary with an AI mishap loses its claim to form people, which is its entire reason to exist.

This asymmetry is the reason the four-stage path exists in this order. It is the reason "move fast and break things" is malpractice for this customer base specifically. It is the reason Safety cannot be optional. **Skipping a stage compounds in a currency these organizations cannot afford to pay.**

#### What we predict happens to organizations that don't start with Safety

Three concrete failure modes, ordered by how often they occur in the field:

**One — an AI-authorship incident.** A parishioner discovers the memorial homily was AI-written. A donor discovers the personalized thank-you was generated. A board member discovers the minutes were AI-summarized with material inaccuracies. The first crisis is almost never the AI use itself — it is the *non-disclosure* of the AI use. The org that disclosed early gets to define the terms; the org that gets caught loses the framing forever.

**Two — a confidential-data exposure.** Pastoral counseling notes pasted into a consumer AI tool for "help drafting follow-up." Donor records pasted in for "personalized appeal generation." Student records pasted in for "letter of recommendation drafting." Each violates pastoral confidentiality norms, donor data agreements, FERPA — and the staff member who did it thought they were being efficient. Without a Guidebook, they do not know it is a violation.

**Three — insurance and regulatory exposure that surfaces only when you need to make a claim.** D&O policies are increasingly carving out AI exclusions. Cyber liability policies are starting to require documented AI governance as a precondition of coverage. State legislation on AI in employment decisions (Colorado SB 205, NYC Local Law 144, Illinois HB 3773) increasingly applies to nonprofits making hiring or eligibility decisions. The org without a Guidebook is uninsured for AI-related incidents and increasingly out of compliance — and does not find out until they need to file a claim or face an audit.

The Guidebook is the document that prevents the unrecoverable version of each of these. **It does not make staff infallible. It does not guarantee safety. But not having one is a guarantee that AI use in the organization is *ungovernable*.**

#### The conveyor belt — why these four stages in this order

The four-stage path is not a taxonomy. It is a chain of consequences. Each stage states the failure mode of the one before it.

**Safety alone is laughable.** You have built barriers against AI without finding out whether it had value in the first place. If that was the goal, you should have just banned the tools. A Guidebook with no Sandbox to test against is theater.

**Sandbox without Safety is reckless.** Testing AI against real work without ratified Rules means every staff member becomes a unilateral decision-maker by default; every vendor pitch lands against a leadership team with no shared frame; every edge case produces a fresh argument. Exploration without guardrails is malpractice given what these tools can touch.

**Skills without Sandbox is abstraction.** You are forming people who have not touched the work in their context against their actual Guidebook. Formation requires friction with the real. Without Sandbox findings, Skills becomes a curriculum delivered into a vacuum.

**Solutions without Skills is replacement.** You have deployed AI into the hands of people who cannot steward it, inside infrastructure you did not rebuild for the new conditions. This is the MIT report's 95% failure mode — but for organizations whose failures cost them their reason to exist.

The conveyor belt is the thesis. We do not push anyone along it; the logic does the moving. The work is ordered because the consequences of skipping a stage compound, and for the organizations we serve, the compound interest is paid in credibility.

#### What each stage produces — concretely

##### Safety
- The **AI Organizational Guidebook**, five layers (Statement, Policy, Context, Rules, Response Plans), seven artifacts
- A board-ratified document defensible to journalists, donors, regulators
- A searchable internal platform where any staff member can find the relevant clause
- The Named Refusals: public covenants the org commits to refuse on principle
- The vendor/tool inventory, data classification, and procurement gate
- The Incident Response Plan with named owners, escalation paths, verification protocols

##### Sandbox
- A **Use Case Portfolio** adjudicated against the Guidebook's Rules — every tested use case marked green, yellow, or red
- A **Discernment Memo** capturing patterns of value, patterns of concern, what the team learned, what the team now believes is true
- A **Readiness Assessment** recommending the team's next stage (Skills, Solutions, both, or neither)
- A team that has *actually used AI against their actual work* — no longer theorizing

##### Skills
- Cohort members with developed **discernment, authorship, and stewardship** capacities
- Curriculum anchored to the org's ratified Statement and Policy from Safety
- A formed group inside the organization that can carry AI decisions across the org without external dependency
- Cohort-only by design: no self-paced version exists, because formation requires community

##### Solutions
- Scoped AI deployment across six configurations (tool optimization, custom recipes, infrastructure builds, domain-specific applications, multi-org coordination, network-scale deployments)
- Built and operated by formed people, governed by the working Guidebook, against use cases the Sandbox already adjudicated
- The technological rebuild AI necessitates, done in the right order

#### The Safety stage in operational detail

Because Safety is the front door, it deserves additional specification.

##### The Guidebook's five layers

**Statement** — *what we believe about AI in relation to our mission.* Two pages. Mission alignment clause, posture toward both fear and uncritical enthusiasm, theological or ethical grounding paragraph, scope clause, annual review cadence. Signed by senior leadership, ratified by the board.

**Policy** — *what we do and what we refuse, operationally.* Sanctioned-use clause, constrained-use clause, prohibited-use clause, consequences clause, scope clause, delegation clause. Includes the **Named Refusals** section — covenants citable when a vendor pitches the prohibited use anyway. Standard refusals: AI in eligibility determinations, AI-driven surveillance of constituents, AI-generated personal voice without disclosure, AI substitution in pastoral or counseling care, deepfakes and voice cloning.

**Context** — *what is actually true in our environment right now.* The Vendor and Tool Inventory: every AI tool in use, by whom, for what, under what vendor terms (training rights on inputs, data residency, retention, enterprise vs. consumer tier). The Data Classification: defined tiers of sensitivity (public, internal, sensitive, confidential) with examples specific to the organization. A procurement gate for new tools. Reviewed quarterly.

**Rules** — *what governs AI use in specific domains.* The Data Handling Rules map data tiers to permitted tools, answering "can I put this in this tool?" for the most common categories of work. The Disclosure & Attribution Rules specify when AI involvement must be disclosed and what language to use. The Care Boundaries specify where AI cannot substitute for human presence regardless of efficiency. Pre-approved workflows give staff a default path that does not require case-by-case judgment.

**Response Plans** — *what we do when something goes wrong.* The Incident Response Plan is required from day one: clear definition of what counts as an incident; named owners with after-hours backup coverage; escalation path (staff → senior leadership → board); pre-arranged verification protocols (code words, callback procedures, dual-authorization for fund transfers above a threshold); constituent-communication template ready to deploy; law-enforcement coordination workflow including IC3 reporting at ic3.gov for AI-enabled fraud; post-incident review process to update Rules, Inventory, and the Plan itself.

##### Two paths through Safety

**SafeGuide** — the free, self-directed path. 33-page Field Guide, all five layers, with checklists. Genuinely sufficient on its own; the trust dividend arrives the moment the board ratifies, regardless of who drafted it. Best fit: leadership teams with time, alignment, internal capacity, and the discipline to finish what they start.

**SafeStart** — the facilitated path. $1,000. Two weeks. Six steps.

1. *Engagement* (Day 0) — org signs; private dashboard provisioned within 24 hours; contributor and decider roles confirmed.
2. *One-hour kickoff Zoom* (within 3 business days) — contributor team (4–7 people across functions) and lead decider meet with Movemental; organizational context, existing AI usage, leadership concerns, organizational artifacts to inform drafting.
3. *Drafting and asynchronous review* (Week One) — Movemental drafts all five layers customized to the organization; published to the private dashboard within 7 days; contributors review asynchronously, leaving comments and proposed edits.
4. *One-hour revision Zoom* (Week Two) — contributor team walks through significant comments, resolves open questions, locks in revisions; revised Guidebook published within 48 hours.
5. *Internal ratification* (end of Week Two) — deciding team meets *internally, without Movemental in the room*, to ratify through whatever governance process the organization requires. The ratification is theirs.
6. *Publication and rollout* (Week Three) — board ratification packet exports from the dashboard; constituent-facing communication template ready; staff onboarding materials available.

**Contributor time commitment:** approximately 3–4 hours per contributor across the two weeks. The total leadership-team time is higher; the per-person commitment is modest by design.

##### What every SafeStart organization gets

- A complete five-layer Guidebook customized to their organization, board-ratified
- A **publishable internal documentation platform** — private dashboard where staff search by question and get the relevant clause; new hires see the org's full AI posture on day one; the board sees role-appropriate views
- **Format flexibility — delivered in whatever format the organization actually uses**: print-quality PDF for board packets and denominational/accreditor filings; HTML and CSS for embedding on the org's website; dashboard for living, searchable internal use
- **Pre-built rollout supports**: board ratification packet, staff announcement templates, constituent-facing communication draft, FAQ for the people the org serves, incident-readiness briefing for senior leadership
- **A pre-engagement communication framework** for talking to staff before the work begins — because quiet engagements produce suspicion, openly named engagements produce buy-in
- **Integration with the rest of the Path** — when the org moves to Sandbox, the Use Case Portfolio is adjudicated against their Guidebook's Rules automatically; when they enter Skills, the formation curriculum anchors to their Statement and Policy. The Guidebook is not a one-time artifact; it is the foundation everything else builds on

##### Free as part of the methodology — the Integrity Diagnostic

Every organization, whether or not they engage Movemental, gets a free **ten-minute, fifty-question Integrity Diagnostic** that maps their existing AI use and shows where the gaps are before they decide which path to take. Real even if they never engage further. This is consistent with the redemptive-pricing posture: the methodology is free, the execution is paid, and the diagnostic is the bridge between them.

#### What the Safety stage is and is not

Safety is **the foundation, not the whole journey.** A ratified Guidebook does not solve the AI problem in the organization. What it does is make governance *possible* — turning informal staff judgment into something the organization has actually decided.

The trust dividend arrives the moment the board ratifies. From that moment, the organization has:

- A public answer when a journalist asks
- A defensible posture when a major donor asks
- A shared frame when staff surface an edge case
- A document that can be cited when a vendor pitches a prohibited use anyway
- Integration into D&O and cyber liability conversations

AI use in the organization is no longer ad hoc. It is governed.

That is why we start here.
