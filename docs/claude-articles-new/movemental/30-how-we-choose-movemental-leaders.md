# How We Choose Movemental Leaders

`movemental-voice-selection-standard.md` · 2,534 words

### How we choose movemental leaders

*A working document for Josh, Brad, and Alan: the definition, the standard, and the process for who joins the network. Written to be adopted, amended, or argued with. It exists so the discernment that has carried us this far can be shared, taught to a third selector, and defended when someone asks why a person is in or out.*

---

#### A note on language

We call them **movemental leaders**, not "Voices." "Voice" works as a gesture toward the collective, but it sounds strange applied to a person, and the language we use about people becomes the posture we hold toward them. "Movement Voices" can stay as the public name of the network. No individual is called a Voice.

---

#### Why this exists

The network has grown through one path: the personal, curated invitation of Brad and Alan. That has worked, and for a reason. It rests on their discernment, their relational knowledge, their taste, and their willingness to stake their own names on each person they bring in. That is the asset. It is not a gap to be fixed.

The gap is that the standard has lived only in their heads. It cannot be taught to a third selector. It cannot be explained to someone who is told no. And it cannot be defended if we are ever asked why a particular person is in and another is out.

This document makes the discernment legible without replacing it. The judgment stays human. What changes is that it now has words, a process, and a memory.

A warning at the top, because it governs everything below. The goal is not to turn discernment into a score. A network curated by trusted people is valuable precisely because a trusted person said yes, one at a time. The day we replace that with a rubric anyone can run, we have built the thing we exist to stand against: legitimacy by metric instead of by trust. The criteria here filter. They never decide. A person decides.

---

#### What a movemental leader is

A movemental leader is someone with a real body of work who actually moves others. Not a commentator on movements, but someone who starts, carries, sends, or multiplies them. Their influence is genuine rather than merely large. A big platform is not the qualification, and a modest one is not a disqualification. What matters is that real people and real work move because of them.

They carry **movemental DNA, whether they name it or not.** This is the heart of the definition, so it needs care.

mDNA is the orientation toward sending and multiplying rather than maintaining, toward forming people rather than running programs, toward decentralized and organic growth, toward incarnational presence, toward the kind of shared risk that turns a group into a movement. Some leaders name all of this in so many words, because they come from the movement-thinking world and use the language fluently. Others will never say "mDNA," may never have read a page of the literature, and will still embody it plainly in what they have built. We are looking for the conviction in the work, not the vocabulary on the page. A youth minister who multiplies young leaders, a social entrepreneur who builds something that sends people out, a justice leader who forms a movement instead of managing a cause, a lawyer who has changed how a whole field works, all can carry mDNA without the word for it. The gate is the reality, not the citation.

And their conduct is such that any of the three of us would personally vouch for them. Not "tolerate." Vouch.

That last part is the spine of the whole thing. Membership is a trust loan. Every existing leader's credibility is quietly lent to every new one. When a reader trusts the network, they are trusting that everyone in it was worth the others standing behind them. So the real question under every decision is never "is this person impressive." It is "would we stake the network's name on them." If the honest answer is no, the rest of the case does not matter.

---

#### A movement, not a guild

This deserves its own section, because it is the easiest thing to get wrong and the hardest to notice while it happens.

The danger, given who we are, is that the network quietly becomes a club of movement thinkers and authors, because that is the world we swim in and the people we most easily see. That would be a failure, not a success. A movement is not a guild of intellectuals.

Movemental leaders are everywhere. In social entrepreneurship. Youth ministry. Justice work. Law. Education. Business. The arts. The unifying thread is mDNA and trust, not a shared profession and not a shared bookshelf. So we actively seek leaders across fields, and treat drift toward a single type, all authors, all one tradition, all one kind of work, as a problem to correct, not a neutral outcome.

This reshapes the third seat, too. The independence we are looking for is not only a different demographic vantage. It is a different field. A selector who lives in the world of practitioners, or justice, or enterprise, will see movemental leaders that two movement-thinkers structurally cannot. We choose the third seat partly for the rooms it opens that the current two do not sit in.

Every nomination can be measured partly by this: does it widen the network into a field the movement-thinking world does not occupy, or does it deepen a corner that is already crowded.

---

#### The standard

A small set of gates that must all be true, and then one judgment no list can make. The gates filter out who clearly does not belong. The judgment decides who does.

##### The gates (all must be true)

1. **A real body of work.** Something they have made, taught, led, or built that exists and can be examined. Not promise, not potential, not a following alone. Work.

2. **Leadership that multiplies.** People have actually followed, multiplied, or been formed under them. They lead leaders, or catalyze movements, or have sent people who now lead. Commentary, however good, is not leadership.

3. **Movemental DNA, explicit or implicit.** The conviction is real in the work, named or not, as described above.

4. **Trustworthy in conduct and character.** No known pattern of conduct, public or private, that the network could not stand behind. This is the one gate where a single credible problem is enough, and where "we are not sure" means we wait, not proceed.

##### The judgment (after the gates, the only thing that decides)

Would we stake the network's name on this person.

That is the whole judgment. A person can clear every gate and still be a no, because the gates detect the floor and the judgment reads the fit. We make it together, as people who know the landscape and will carry the consequence. It is deliberately not a checklist, and it must stay that way.

---

#### What we do and do not gatekeep

The network's collateral is trust and movemental DNA. It is not doctrinal agreement and it is not a political coalition. This matters enough to say flatly.

**We do not gatekeep on the liberal/conservative spectrum.** The network spans it on purpose. A network that admits only one side becomes a tribe, and a tribe is not a movement. Members will disagree, sometimes sharply, on contested political and theological questions, and that breadth is a feature, not a tolerance. It is part of what would make this a movement across the church and the wider world rather than a faction within it.

**We do not gatekeep on contested doctrine or denominational distinctives.** People who land differently on debated questions can both belong. The network is not a statement of faith and should never become one.

**We gatekeep on conduct and character, not belief.** We do not admit people whose conduct the network cannot stand behind: abuse, exploitation, dishonesty, or a posture toward people that dehumanizes or targets them. The line is never "they believe something we disagree with." The line is "their conduct, or their core posture toward people, is something we cannot underwrite with our own names." We admit people we disagree with. We do not admit people we cannot trust.

**One boundary we have not yet set: the faith floor.** In the mDNA frame, the center is Jesus. If that center is essential to what we mean by a movemental leader, then the network is, at root, oriented to Christian movement, even as it spans every tradition and politics within that. If we mean something broader, that the sending, multiplying, incarnational, forming pattern can be recognized and honored even in leaders outside the faith, then the net is wider and includes the social entrepreneur and the justice leader who never name Jesus but whose work carries the pattern. This is the deepest scope decision in front of us. The "explicit or implicit" mDNA gate and the "movement, not a guild" principle both push toward the wider net. The mDNA frame itself pulls toward the Christological center. We have to decide where that line sits. Everything else here works either way.

#### The process

Short on purpose, and its load-bearing rule is not the nomination. It is the dissent.

1. **Nomination.** Any of us may nominate. We write a short case: who the person is, how they meet the definition, and the plain answer to "would we stake the network's name on them, and why." Where relevant, we note which field the nominee widens the network into.

2. **The written-reasons rule.** Anyone who believes a nominee does not belong must say so in writing, and must say why. A no is never a shrug or a veto by mood. It is a reason, on the record. This single rule does most of the work in this document. It turns taste into reasons. It builds a memory of precedent, so the standard sharpens over time. It makes a no as accountable as a yes.

3. **Consent, not vote.** A nominee is accepted unless one of us raises a substantive, written objection. An unresolved serious objection blocks. We do not admit by majority. One credible "I would not stake my name on this person" should be able to stop an admission, because the entire value of the network is that every member would.

4. **The record.** Every nomination, case, and objection is kept. Over time the record becomes the truest definition, because it is made of real decisions. We revisit this written definition against the record once a year and correct the words to match the discernment.

##### When an objection is personal

The written-reasons rule has a failure mode and a strength in the same place: objections rooted in personal history. One of us may have history with a nominee. An old disagreement. A falling-out. A friction that never healed. Two things have to be kept apart.

A personal disagreement or preference is **not** a reason to block. We are not building a network of people we would each choose as friends, and a difference of opinion, even a sharp one, is not a trust problem. A theological or strategic disagreement is not a trust problem either.

But sometimes a personal disagreement is exactly where a real trust issue first showed itself, because we saw, up close, something about how this person handles power, conflict, money, truth, or vulnerable people. That is not preference. That is evidence, and often the truest evidence there is.

So when an objection comes from personal history, the rule is: name it as personal, then say which kind it is. Is this "I would not choose to work with them," or is it "the network cannot stake its name on them, and here is the conduct that tells me so." Only the second blocks. The test is whether the disagreement bears on character and the network's collateral, or on taste, style, and old wounds. The one objecting discloses the history, so the other two can weigh the objection knowing where it comes from. Where the history is heavy enough that we cannot tell which kind it is, we say that too, and lean on each other to test it.

The point is not to disqualify personal knowledge. Personal knowledge is often the truest knowledge. The point is to keep a grudge from wearing the costume of a principle.

#### The selectors

There are three of us. For now there are two, Brad and Alan, who share a vantage and, more to the point, share a field. The third seat exists to break both kinds of sameness: a different vantage, and a different world. We choose the third selector for the independence of their judgment and the reach of their relational knowledge into rooms, and fields, the current two do not occupy. If the right person is a woman of color, that is most likely not incidental. She sees a part of the landscape the current two structurally cannot. We choose for the vantage, name the reason openly, and it stops being a checkbox.

All three hold the same power: to nominate, and to block with a written reason. The seat is a position of trust, not seniority.

---

#### Two things to settle before there is a person they apply to

**Guard against incentive drift.** The day membership has clear value, citation, credibility transfer, reach, there will be pressure to admit people who help the network's numbers rather than people who meet the definition. This is the specific way a trust network dies: slowly lowering the bar for growth. The written-reasons rule is the defense, but we name the risk out loud here so it is never a surprise. Our own thesis, claim only what is earned, has to apply hardest to who we let in.

**Decide the exit before we need it.** A network that can only add cannot protect its standard, because the cost of a wrong yes is unbounded if there is no way out. We agree, now, on what would cause someone to no longer belong: an integrity failure, a drift from the DNA, public conduct the network cannot underwrite. And on who decides, which is the same three of us, by the same written-reasons standard. The terms need not be published. They need to exist. It is far easier to write the exit rule before there is a specific person it applies to.

---

#### Open decisions for the three of us

1. **The faith floor.** The biggest one, named in the boundaries section. Is the network Christian at its root, or wider than that.

2. **The third seat.** Who holds it, chosen for a different vantage and a different field.

3. **The exit terms.** What would actually cause someone to no longer belong.

4. **Test it against ourselves.** Read this definition against the leaders already in the network. If anyone already in would not pass it, the definition is wrong, not the person, and we fix the words until they describe the people we already trust.
