# Market Segmentation & EEAT: Modular Capability Architecture

`market-segmentation-modular-edition.md` · 3,247 words

### Market Segmentation & EEAT — Modular Capability Edition

*Converted from the original "org-type → screens" version. This edition organizes everything around **one capability menu** — the slots a buyer already shops for — and then shows what lights up per org family and per shape. It is neither a CMS nor a CRM; those are two doors on the same menu.*

---

#### 0. How to read this document

The old version listed, for each kind of org, a long set of Public and Staff/admin **screens**. That answered "what pages exist" but not "what am I actually buying." Buyers don't shop for screens. They shop for a **capability they already have a word for** — "I need a donor database," "I need to send a newsletter," "I need the AI thing."

So this edition flips it:

1. **One master menu** of capabilities (the categories everyone recognizes), each with modules and sub-modules.
2. **Per family** (nonprofit, church, institution), we mark each capability **Core / Available / Rare / —** and say what's inside it for them.
3. **Within each family**, the old "types" become **configurations** — not separate products, just *which modules light up*.

The menu is universal. The configuration is the product.

---

#### 1. The segmentation rule (unchanged — this still governs everything)

The segmentation variable changes at every stage, and the nonprofit/church/institution label is the right variable at only **one** of the four. Keep the method universal; segment by the thing actually doing the work:

- **Safety** → segments by **data sensitivity, population served, regulation** — not the org-type label. (High-sensitivity data and vulnerable populations are the one place generic is genuinely dangerous.)
- **Sandbox** → segments only by **example tasks**. Same posture for a pastor, a development director, a dean. A small library of starting tasks, nothing structural.
- **Training** → segments by **role** (comms, development, programs, admin, senior leader). The three labels are nearly noise here.
- **Tech** → segments **structurally**, by **functional axes + module selection**, with a **vocabulary skin** on top. This is the only stage where the three labels even approximate the right cut — and even here the real driver is the axes underneath (single-org vs. network, do they form people, publish vs. report, revenue model).

**The rule on the wall:** segmentation lives in the content and configuration, never in the methodology. The three labels are a **front door**, not a product architecture. Underneath, the product is *one path + one spine + a configured module library.*

A note on the two layers at Tech, because they cost very differently:

- **The vocabulary skin** (cheap) — donors vs. members vs. students; giving vs. tithing vs. tuition. Makes the platform *feel* tailored.
- **The module selection** (expensive) — which capabilities are actually on. Makes the platform *be* tailored.

You want both. Don't confuse them.

#### 2. The master capability menu (the universal SSOT)

Ten categories. The first four are **doorways** — the buyer already has the word, so that's where they self-locate and where the sale happens. The next four are the **foundation** — the buyer can't name them yet, but they're what make this one connected platform instead of a pile of disconnected point tools. Then a practical shelf, and one structural add-on.

##### Doorways (the buyer walks in asking for these)

**Publication — your publishing engine** *(the WordPress + Mailchimp slot; fine to call it the CMS)*
- **Website & pages** — your public site, editable by you.
- **Articles** — long-form writing. *Sub: pillar pieces · cluster pieces · essays / commentary.*
- **Stories** — impact and program stories that show what you actually did.
- **Newsletter / email** — what you send to your list. *Sub: broadcasts · automated sequences (welcome, re-engage) · segments · public archive.*
- **Media library** — audio/video with transcripts (sermons, talks, podcasts). *Sub: upload · auto-transcribe · publish · related content.*
- **Research / publications** — for orgs whose product is ideas: papers with citations.

**Formation — your teaching engine** *(the Teachable / Thinkific / Circle slot; the LMS)*
- **Pathways** — guided journeys, lighter than a full course.
- **Courses & lessons** — the actual course player.
- **Cohorts** — running a group through together. *Sub: scheduling · rosters · facilitator tools · completion.*
- **Community** — discussion spaces for participants.
- **Assessments & reflection** — checks and prompts inside the learning.
- **Certificates** — proof of completion.
- **Enroll / apply** — how people get in.

**Relationships — your CRM** *(the Neon / Bloomerang slot; the "customer engine")*
- **Contact records** — one page per person, full history.
- **Segments & lists** — group people by what they've done or who they are.
- **Stewardship pipelines** — move someone from first touch to committed. *Sub: donor journeys · visitor→member→leader (church) · lapsed re-engagement.*
- **Communications log** — every touch in one place.
- *(Church skin of the same engine: members, households, attendance — what they'd otherwise buy as a ChMS.)*

**Giving — your fundraising engine** *(the Givebutter / Donorbox / Classy slot)*
- **Donations** — one-time and recurring.
- **Campaigns** — time-bound pushes with a goal and a progress bar.
- **Pledges** — commitments over time.
- **Receipts & statements** — what donors and your bookkeeper need.
- **Store / e-commerce** — sell books, merch, resources.
- **Paid membership / subscriptions** — recurring access or dues.

##### Foundation (comes attached to the doorway; the buyer feels it but can't name it)

**AI Assistant — your agent platform** *(the new slot: ChatGPT, but it knows your org)*
- **Public assistant** — answers visitors: "what do you do, what's your impact?"
- **Staff assistants** — draft the grant report, turn one event into a newsletter and three posts, answer the repeat question, find the 2019 document.
- **Smart search** — ask your whole library a question and get a cited answer back.

**Library — everything you've ever made, gathered and searchable** *(the slot most orgs don't know they're missing)*
- **Bring it in** — import documents, reports, archives; transcribe audio and video.
- **Search** — one box across all of it.
- **Public index** — the browsable front of it.
- *(This is what we used to call "corpus." A buyer doesn't shop for "corpus ingestion" — they feel it as "finally, one place where all our stuff lives and can be found." It's also what makes the AI Assistant and the Publication engine actually work, so it's foundation, not a side feature.)*

**Visibility & Trust — being found and believed by Google and AI** *(the SEO slot, extended)*
- **Search & AI visibility** — structured so Google *and* AI answer engines get you right (SEO plus the newer "GEO").
- **Sources & citations** — every claim backed by a source.
- **Real profiles** — your people and your org as entities machines recognize.
- **Network & endorsements** — who cites you and vouches for you.

**Governance — your AI rulebook, live** *(a slot nobody really sells yet)*
- **AI Handbook** — your policy (what you'll do, what you refuse), live and searchable, not a PDF in a drawer.
- **Roles & permissions** — who can do what.
- **Analytics** — what's working.
- **Readiness check** — where your team actually is.

##### The practical shelf

**Operations**
- **Events & registration.**
- **Volunteers** — opportunities, shifts, hours.
- **Forms & intake** — applications, requests, sign-ups.
- **Grants** — pipeline, deliverables, deadlines, and report drafting (leans on the AI Assistant).

##### The structural add-on

**Network — HQ plus chapters** *(the association-management slot; only for parent orgs and denominations)*
- **Member sub-sites** — each affiliate gets its own mini-platform.
- **Provisioning & templates** — stand up a new member from a default.
- **Roll-up analytics & governance** — see and steward across all of them.

> **The four foundation categories (AI Assistant, Library, Visibility & Trust, Governance) are always on.** They are the spine. Every build includes them no matter the family. The doorways and the shelf are what get configured.

**Legend for the tables below:** **Core** = on by default for this family · **Available** = turn on if it fits · **Rare** = occasional · **—** = not typical.

#### 3. Nonprofit — *"be understood and funded"*

**Vocabulary skin:** donors · giving · grants · impact · programs · volunteers.
**What they walk in asking for:** a donor database (Relationships), a way to take donations (Giving), a newsletter and a website (Publication), and increasingly "an AI thing" (AI Assistant). Those are the doorways. Everything else comes attached.

| Capability | Nonprofit baseline | What lights up |
|---|---|---|
| **Publication** | Core | Website & pages, **Stories** (impact), Newsletter/email. Articles available. |
| **Relationships (CRM)** | Core | Donor/stakeholder records, segments, **stewardship pipelines** (donor journeys, lapsed re-engagement), comms log. |
| **Giving** | Core | Donations (recurring + one-time), campaigns, pledges, receipts. |
| **AI Assistant** | Core (foundation) | Public "what do you do / what's your impact" agent; staff assistants (draft the grant report, event → posts, find the document). |
| **Library** | Core (foundation) | Impact history, program records, reports, board minutes — gathered, searchable, cited. |
| **Visibility & Trust** | Core (foundation) | Verifiable provenance on outcomes and claims; people and org as real entities; optional network place. |
| **Governance** | Core (foundation) | The AI Handbook live, with donor-data and beneficiary-data lines drawn; roles; analytics; readiness. |
| **Operations** | Core | **Grants** (pipeline + agent-drafted reports), events & registration, volunteers, forms/intake. |
| **Formation (LMS)** | Available | Only if they deliver programs or training (→ becomes Core in the formation shape). |
| **Network** | Rare | Only for the network/intermediary shape. |
| *Giving → Store / subscriptions* | Rare | Occasional resource sales. |

**The four nonprofit shapes — same menu, different modules lit:**

- **Direct-service / program** *(shelter, food bank, clinic — the default)*. The baseline exactly as above: Relationships + Giving + Publication(stories) + Operations(grants), foundation underneath. Formation light or off; Network off.
- **Formation / program-delivering** *(Youthfront-type)*. **Formation flips to Core** — pathways, courses, cohorts, certificates, enroll/apply become the center of gravity. Relationships shifts toward a **learner CRM**; Giving gets lighter; grants usually stay. This is the one nonprofit shape that leans toward the church/institution build.
- **Think-tank / thought-leadership** *(research org, policy shop, author-built ministry)*. **Publication → research/publications** and **Library + Visibility & Trust** become the center — papers, citations, the credibility graph. Giving often shifts to **membership/subscriptions**. Relationships stays light; Formation off. This is *institution-lite* — it barely behaves like a service nonprofit.
- **Network / intermediary** *(grantmaker, association, parent org)*. **Network flips to Core** — member sub-sites, provisioning, roll-up analytics and governance. Operations' grants becomes a full **grantmaking pipeline**. Multi-tenant; shares its build with denominations, not with shelters.

#### 4. Church — *"reach, disciple, shepherd"*

**Vocabulary skin:** members · households · tithing/giving · groups · next steps · attendance.
**What they walk in asking for:** church management (Relationships, ChMS-grade), giving, the sermon archive (Publication → media library), and discipleship (Formation). Four doorways, and unusually for this family, **two of them — Formation and the media Library — are non-negotiable**, because discipleship and the sermon corpus *are* the church's core product.

| Capability | Church baseline | What lights up |
|---|---|---|
| **Publication** | Core | **Media library** (sermons: audio/video + transcript) is central — this is the church's corpus surface. Plus website, ministries pages, newsletter. |
| **Formation (LMS)** | Core | Discipleship pathways, classes, next-steps journeys. Rarely optional — discipleship *is* the product. |
| **Relationships (CRM)** | Core | **ChMS-grade**: members, households, attendance, groups, **assimilation pipeline** (visitor→member→leader), care/follow-up (with data-sensitivity guardrails). |
| **Giving** | Core | Recurring, pledges, campaigns. |
| **AI Assistant** | Core (foundation) | Sermon → weekly study/devotional/posts, generate the bulletin, answer repeat questions, public agent grounded in actual teaching. |
| **Library** | Core (foundation) | Every sermon and teaching transcribed, searchable, cited. |
| **Visibility & Trust** | Core (foundation) | Teaching verifiably theirs; people as entities; faithful representation (never words in a pastor's mouth). |
| **Governance** | Core (foundation) | The Handbook live with the **pastoral and member-data lines** drawn (AI + counseling, AI + the sermon, disclosure to the congregation). |
| **Operations** | Available | Events & registration, groups admin, prayer/care requests (forms), app/notifications. |
| **Network** | Available | Core in the multi-site shape. |
| *Voice modules* | Available | If the pastor is also an author — see the teaching-church shape. |
| *Giving → Store / paid courses* | Rare | Merch, resources, paid classes. |

**The three church shapes:**

- **Single congregation.** The baseline above — and the most "fused" product on the whole menu (church management + publishing + formation + agents in one). The default church shape.
- **Multi-site / network church.** Single-congregation modules **plus Network → Core**: per-campus sub-sites, a shared teaching publisher, a multi-campus dashboard, network governance. Same multi-tenant requirement as a denomination, smaller scale.
- **Teaching church / pastor-as-author.** The whole single-congregation build **plus a second Voice stack** scoped to the pastor as a public author: Publication (their articles, books, corpus), Formation (their pathways and courses), an AI Lab in their voice, an author newsletter, and a Visibility & Trust author-entity (credentials, sameAs, place in the network). Two products in one.

#### 5. Institution — *"credibility and coherence"*

**Vocabulary skin:** students · faculty · alumni · tuition/advancement · programs · credentials · departments.
**What they walk in asking for:** an LMS and credentialing (Formation), admissions (Operations), and — uniquely to this family — **verifiability and governance at scale**, which they often *can* name because it's their whole reason to be here. This is the heaviest version of the foundation: the spine isn't background, it's the point.

| Capability | Institution baseline | What lights up |
|---|---|---|
| **Visibility & Trust** | Core — *the crux* | Verifiable provenance **at scale** (distinguishable from a well-made fake); the credential and canon made coherent; the credibility graph. This is the institution's entire reason to be here. |
| **Governance** | Core — *central, not background* | The Handbook across departments, AI policy and ratification, multi-department roles. |
| **Library** | Core — *the heaviest* | Decades of scholarship, archives, faculty work, committee records — gathered, deduplicated, searchable, cited into one source. |
| **AI Assistant** | Core (foundation) | Scholarship-bounded agents, governance/policy assistants, a public agent that represents the institution accurately. |
| **Publication** | Core | Scholarship/publications index, cited paper pages with provenance. |
| **Formation (LMS)** | Core *if teaching* | LMS + **credentialing**: courses, programs, continuing ed, certificates, transcripts. |
| **Operations** | Core *if teaching* | **Admissions / application management**; events/lectures. |
| **Relationships (CRM)** | Available | Alumni/advancement CRM; student records (with academic-records guardrails). |
| **Giving** | Available | Advancement / giving. |
| *Giving → Membership / subscriptions* | Available → Core for associations | Journals, denominational membership, paywall. |
| **Network** | Available → central for denominations | Multi-tenant: propagate a platform to every church/school/department underneath. |

**The three institution shapes:**

- **Seminary / school (teaching institution).** Corpus + verifiability + governance Core, **plus Formation/credentialing and Admissions → Core**. Faculty as entities; student records under academic guardrails; alumni/advancement available. The teaching-institution shape.
- **Denomination / network body.** **Network → Core**, and the most demanding case: governance-at-scale plus propagating a platform to every member church (each member church gets the **single-congregation church build** as its sub-site). Add credentialing/ordination where applicable. This is the "two timelines" turned into product. Shares its spine with multi-site churches and network nonprofits.
- **Association / accreditor / publisher / journal.** **Giving → membership/subscriptions becomes Core** (often with a paywall); Publication runs an **editorial / peer-review workflow** (submission → review → publish); verifiability Core; accreditation admin lives in Operations/forms. Light on Formation. The credibility-and-membership shape.

#### 6. Individual Voice — the reference implementation *(the Alan platform)*

Kept because it's the build everything else was reverse-engineered from, and the teaching-church shape reuses it wholesale.

**Identity:** *"be coherent, found, and connected."* **Doorways:** Publication + Formation, on the foundation.

- **Publication** — Core: pillar/cluster articles, essays, newsletter.
- **Formation** — Core: pathways + courses + cohorts (the formation engine).
- **Library** — Core (foundation): the full corpus (books, talks, articles) indexed, cited, unique-intent-per-URL.
- **AI Assistant** — Core (foundation): the AI Lab agent, corpus-bounded and voice-configured; a public agent in the author's voice.
- **Visibility & Trust** — Core (foundation): the voice constitution, the author as a real entity, the scenius cross-citation graph (you're a node).
- **Giving** — Available: paid subscriptions/membership, paid courses, store (books/resources).
- **Relationships** — Rare: beyond a subscriber list. **Operations** — Rare: occasional events.

#### 7. EEAT — the Visibility & Trust + Library + AI Assistant categories, in action

The EEAT work isn't a separate product; it's what the foundation categories *produce*. Both flows below run the same spine: **person/org entity → gathered corpus → cited claims → endorsements → network position.** Experience and Expertise come from the body of work being real and gathered (Library). Authoritativeness comes from entities and who-cites-whom (Visibility & Trust). Trust comes from provenance, refusals, and how-we-use-AI (Governance + Visibility & Trust).

##### A. About Movemental & the founders

**Public-facing**
1. About Movemental (org overview) — Authoritativeness
2. Why we exist / the thesis (fragmentation + AI) — Expertise
3. How we use AI on ourselves — Trust *(we eat our own cooking)*
4. What we refuse to do (the refusals) — Trust
5. Founders index (Alan, Brad, Josh) — Authoritativeness
6. Founder profile ×3 — Experience + Expertise
7. The story / origin (the two-year conversation, 2026) — Experience
8. Our research / writing index — Expertise
9. Research / paper page (cited, provenance) — Expertise + Trust
10. Endorsements / who trusts us — Authoritativeness
11. The network / scenius overview — Authoritativeness
12. Security & data trust — Trust
13. Contact / talk to us — Trust

**Entity / machine layer** — 14. Org entity + schema (Organization, sameAs) — Authoritativeness · 15. Founder person-entities + sameAs — Authoritativeness · 16. Citations layer (every claim sourced) — Trust

*The founder flow leans on the refusals and "we use it on ourselves."*

##### B. About a Movement Leader / Voice

**Public-facing**
1. Voice profile / home — Experience + Authoritativeness
2. Their corpus index (books, talks, articles gathered) — Expertise
3. Corpus item page — Experience
4. Article / essay index — Expertise
5. Article page (cited, unique-intent-per-URL) — Expertise + Trust
6. Pathways index — Experience
7. Pathway page — Experience
8. Courses index + course page — Expertise
9. The AI Lab (their corpus-bounded agent) — Experience *(the "talk to the work" proof)*
10. Citations / sources — Trust
11. Who cites them / endorsements — Authoritativeness
12. Their place in the network (cross-citation) — Authoritativeness
13. Newsletter / subscribe — Trust *(direct relationship)*

**Entity / machine layer** — 14. Person entity + credentials + sameAs (ORCID, VIAF, etc.) — Authoritativeness · 15. Author-to-work links — Expertise + Trust · 16. Cross-citation graph (the scenius edges) — Authoritativeness

*The Voice flow leans on the corpus and the AI Lab.*
