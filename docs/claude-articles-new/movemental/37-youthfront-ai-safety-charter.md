## AI Organizational Statement

What we believe about AI in relation to our mission.

1Mission alignment

Youthfront exists to bring youth into a growing relationship with Jesus Christ. For more than eighty years the work has centered on prayer, community, and service -- curating environments where young people discover, explore, and experience God. That work is relational at its core. It happens in the presence of a camp counselor at LaCygne, a mentor in the Argentine neighborhood, a youth worker being trained and encouraged, a young person being seen.

We adopt AI tools only where they serve that work, and never where they displace it. AI may carry the administrative weight that keeps ministry running -- the scheduling, the drafting, the paperwork -- so that our people have more of themselves to give to the young people in front of them. It may not stand in for the presence of a person where presence is the point. The test for any AI use at Youthfront is simple: does this free us to be more present, or does it quietly replace the presence itself? 

2Our posture

We are neither fearful nor uncritical about AI.

We refuse fear-driven avoidance, because our staff are already using these tools and pretending otherwise leaves them without guidance. We equally refuse uncritical enthusiasm, because efficiency is not our mission and speed is not a virtue in formation. AI, used with discernment and under clear rules, can free Youthfront to do more of the work it exists to do. Used carelessly, it can erode the very things -- attention, presence, trust -- that our work is made of. We hold both truths at once, which is the same posture our staff described when we asked them: open and cautious, curious and worried, in the same breath.

3Categories of care

There are kinds of work at Youthfront where efficiency will never be the deciding criterion, because a human being is the irreducible thing being offered. These include:

  * Spiritual care, mentoring, and pastoral conversation with young people
  * The formation of campers, and the formation of youth workers
  * Relational ministry with vulnerable youth, including young people in juvenile detention and teen parents
  * The supervision, safety, and care of children entrusted to us

In these categories, AI does not substitute for a person. Our staff named these same categories, nearly unanimously, when asked what should be off-limits to AI. This Statement makes their instinct our conviction.

4Theological and ethical grounding

Youthfront's identity is contemplative formation: attention, presence, and the slow, unhurried work of discipleship. We have spent years recovering classic Christian formation and building a praying community at LaCygne shaped by that conviction. That same conviction shapes how we hold AI. Formation cannot be automated. Presence cannot be delegated. The pace of these tools is fast; the pace of the soul is slow; and where the two meet, we choose the soul.CONFIRMConfirmWhether Youthfront has a formal written statement of faith or doctrinal statement, and the specific theological tradition and language Youthfront's leadership wants this grounding paragraph to rest on. 5Scope

This Statement applies to Youthfront staff, seasonal camp staff, volunteers, contractors, and vendors using AI on Youthfront's behalf, and it informs the work of the Board of Directors. It applies with particular force to anyone whose work touches children or children's data.

## AI Organizational Policy

What we will do, and what we refuse to do, operationally.

The Policy translates the Statement into practice. It is the document a new staff member or seasonal camp hire reads in their first week, and the document a parent, a donor, or a partner church reads when they ask what Youthfront's position on AI is.

1Sanctioned use

AI assistance is welcomed in low-stakes internal work that touches no children's data and no confidential information:

  * Drafting and editing internal documents, memos, and outlines
  * Research and summarizing public or internal non-sensitive material
  * Scheduling, formatting, and administrative organization
  * Analysis of non-sensitive operational data

This is the category our staff most wanted help with. When asked what they would gladly hand off, they named email, expense reports, scheduling, and data entry. Those are sanctioned.

2Constrained use

AI is permitted in the following categories only with conditions -- disclosure, human supervision, and editorial review by a named, accountable person:

  * Public-facing content: newsletters, social media, website copy, marketing
  * Fundraising and donor communications
  * Curriculum, teaching, and formation resources
  * Any communication sent in a specific person's name or voice
  * Analysis that touches Sensitive-tier data (see the Context page)

The conditions for each are specified in the Rules (Page 4). Constrained use is never a default; it requires the review step named there.

3Prohibited use

AI is not permitted in these categories, regardless of how efficient or impressive a tool appears:

  * Any use in the categories of care named in the Statement -- spiritual care, mentoring, pastoral conversation, the formation of youth, and relational ministry with vulnerable young people
  * Any processing of children's data through a tool that has not been approved under these Rules
  * Any of the Named Refusals below

4Consequences

A violation of this Policy is handled through Youthfront's existing staff-conduct and disciplinary process, proportionate to the seriousness of the violation, with special gravity attached to any violation involving children's data or the categories of care.CONFIRMConfirmYouthfront's actual staff-conduct / HR / disciplinary process and who administers it, so consequences tie to the real process rather than a new one. Confirm whether seasonal camp staff and volunteers are covered by the same process or a separate one.

5Scope

This Policy applies to all Youthfront employees, seasonal camp staff, volunteers, and contractors, and to any vendor using AI on Youthfront's behalf.

6Delegation

Interpretation of edge cases and the granting of exceptions rests with a named role, not an individual, so the authority outlasts any one person. Exceptions are recorded in writing with the reason and the date.CONFIRMConfirmWhich role holds interpretation-and-exception authority -- likely the COO or the Executive VP of Strategic Initiatives -- and whether a small standing group (rather than one person) should hold it. This role is referenced again in the Rules' exception process; keep it consistent.

7Named Refusals

These are covenants, not predictions. Youthfront commits to refuse each of the following on principle, and will cite these refusals when a vendor or tool proposes the use anyway.

  1. **No AI in eligibility determinations.** We do not use AI to decide who receives camp scholarships, financial aid, program admission, or any Youthfront service.
  2. **No behavioral surveillance or profiling of the youth we serve.** We do not use AI to monitor, score, profile, or differentiate the treatment of young people.
  3. **No undisclosed AI-generated personal voice.** We do not send communications -- appeals, letters, pastoral notes -- written by AI in a real person's voice without disclosing it.
  4. **No AI substitution in care.** We do not let AI stand in for a human being in spiritual care, mentoring, crisis support, or the formation relationships at the heart of our work.
  5. **No deepfakes, voice cloning, or undisclosed chatbots** representing Youthfront, its leaders, or its staff.
  6. **No AI substitution for the human supervision of minors, and no AI surveillance, profiling, or depiction of minors.** This refusal is specific to Youthfront because minors are our primary constituents, including in overnight residential settings. It is the refusal we hold most firmly.

Confirm also whether donor appeals or other communications currently go out in a named leader's voice (Mike King's or others'), which determines how refusal 3 and the disclosure rules apply.

What is at stake without this Policy: shadow use. Staff use whatever is convenient because no one has named what is acceptable, and by the time the question is asked, the practice is already entrenched.

Procurement gate

No new AI tool enters this inventory until: (1) a staff member proposes it in writing with the intended use; (2) the delegated role reviews the vendor's terms for training rights, data residency, and retention; (3) the tool is checked against the Data Classification below to confirm which tier of data, if any, it may touch; (4) the delegated role approves, and approval adds the tool to this inventory. A tool that fails any step is not used.

Review cadence

This inventory is reviewed quarterly by the delegated role, with a standing instruction to look for shadow use -- tools staff have started using without adding them here.

BData Classification

Four tiers of sensitivity:

  * **Public** -- information already published or intended for the public.
  * **Internal** -- information for staff use that would cause no harm if seen, but is not published.
  * **Sensitive** -- information that would cause harm, embarrassment, or a breach of trust if exposed.
  * **Confidential** -- information whose exposure would cause serious harm, legal liability, or a violation of trust with a child, a family, a donor, or the organization.

## AI Organizational Rules

What governs AI use in specific domains.

The Policy says what Youthfront does. The Rules say how, specifically, in the three areas where the stakes are highest: **data, disclosure, and care.**

AData Handling Rules

1 * Tier-to-tool mapping (from the Data Classification on Page 3)

  * **Public** data -> any tool.
  * **Internal** data -> approved enterprise tools with acceptable vendor terms only.
  * **Sensitive** data -> approved enterprise tools only, with the review step; never a consumer tool.
  * **Confidential** data -> **no external AI tool, ever.** This includes all children's data without exception.

2 * "Can I put this in this tool?" -- common cases

  * _Summarize a board document?_ Only with an approved enterprise tool; board materials are Sensitive. Redact any Confidential detail first.
  * _Draft a newsletter or social post?_ Yes -- this is Constrained use. Draft with an approved tool, then a named person reviews and the disclosure rule below applies.
  * _Analyze a donor spreadsheet?_ Only with an approved enterprise tool, and only after removing anything Confidential. Donor giving records are Sensitive.
  * _Anything involving a camper 's name, health, behavior, or family?_ **No.** This is Confidential and never goes into an external tool.
  * _Draft an internal staff memo?_ Yes -- Sanctioned use, no disclosure needed.

3 * Pre-approved workflows (default safe paths)

  * _Drafting an internal memo or outline:_ use an approved tool freely; no children's or Confidential data; no disclosure needed.
  * _Preparing a public newsletter or social post:_ draft with an approved tool -> a named staff member edits and approves -> apply the disclosure marker below before publishing.
  * _Summarizing a public report or article:_ use an approved tool freely.

4 * Procurement-review process

Any new tool follows the procurement gate on Page 3, and the delegated role approves before use.

5 * Exception process

A case not covered here is brought to the delegated role named in the Policy's delegation clause, who decides and records the decision in writing with the date and reason.

6 * Breach-reporting process

Any suspected exposure of Sensitive or Confidential data through an AI tool is reported immediately under the Incident Response Plan on Page 5. Reporting a mistake honestly is always treated better than hiding it.CONFIRMConfirmThe real owners of the exception and breach-reporting processes, and any additional common work categories staff want a clear yes/no/with-conditions answer for.

BDisclosure & Attribution Rules

1 * No disclosure needed

  * Formatting, scheduling, spelling and grammar
  * Summarizing internal documents for internal use
  * Drafting internal memos and notes

2 * Disclosure required -- when AI involvement touches

  * Personal communication and correspondence
  * Pastoral or mentoring communication with a young person or family
  * Fundraising appeals or donor communication in a leader's or staff member's voice
  * Public-facing content: newsletters, website, social media, published resources

3 * The disclosure marker

Where disclosure is required, use plain language such as: _" Prepared with AI assistance and reviewed by [name]."_ For a leader-voiced communication, the person whose name appears must have read and approved it personally.

4 * Tiered disclosure

A light touch -- grammar, formatting -- needs no marker. Substantive AI drafting of anything public or personal needs the marker and a named human reviewer. Anything in the categories of care needs no marker because it is not permitted at all.

5 * Attribution for published content

When Youthfront publishes content AI helped generate in a substantive way, the byline reflects the accountable human author, and the disclosure marker makes the AI assistance plain.

6 * Edge cases

  * _AI in curriculum or formation-resource preparation:_ research and structuring are Constrained use with review; the formation content itself carries the theological weight of a named human author.?Leadership's line between acceptable AI help in resource preparation and the point at which formation content must be human-authored.
  * _AI in donor segmentation:_ permitted with an approved enterprise tool on de-identified or Sensitive-tier data under review; never on Confidential data.
  * _AI in board materials:_ summarizing is permitted with an approved tool; redact Confidential detail first.

CCare Boundaries

These are the categories where the presence of a human being is the irreducible input. Each boundary carries a reason drawn from the Statement. Youthfront's own staff named nearly all of these when asked what should be off-limits to AI.

1 * Irreducible human presence -- AI may not substitute here

  * The initial pastoral or spiritual-care conversation with a young person after grief, trauma, or crisis
  * Mentoring and discipleship relationships with campers and youth
  * Crisis first contact with a young person in distress
  * Any care relationship with vulnerable youth, including those in juvenile detention and teen parents
  * The direct supervision and safety of children
  * Disciplinary or difficult conversations with staff

_Rationale: the Statement holds that formation cannot be automated and presence cannot be delegated. In these moments a young person needs a person, not a tool ._

2 * Secondary cases -- AI may support a human but may not replace one

  * A follow-up note after a pastoral conversation (the conversation itself was human; the note may be drafted with help and then personally written or approved by the staff member)
  * Intake or registration forms (AI may help build the form; a human reviews every child's information)
  * General follow-up communication to families (drafted with help, reviewed and sent by a named person)

3 * Named workflow for each secondary case

In every secondary case, a named, accountable staff member reviews, personalizes, and takes responsibility before anything reaches a young person or family. "AI drafts, a human edits" is not enough on its own -- the human must be named and accountable.CONFIRMConfirmFor each secondary case, who the accountable staff member is by role. Confirm also whether Youthfront camps have on-site counselors or clinicians and documented crisis protocols, which would add clinical-intake and mental-health boundaries here.

4 * Review process for new categories

As AI capabilities grow, new kinds of work will appear that these boundaries did not anticipate. Before AI is used in any new category that touches young people or care, it is brought to the delegated role and tested against the Statement's categories of care.

What is at stake without this page: a slow displacement of human presence in exactly the places where Youthfront's reason to exist lives; disclosure lapses that erode trust before anyone notices; and data exposure that becomes a breach.

## AI Organizational Response Plans

What we do when something goes wrong.

The Rules describe steady-state behavior. This page describes what Youthfront does when steady state fails. The **Incident Response Plan** is required from day one.

AIncident Response Plan

1 * What counts as an incident

  * Harmful or inaccurate AI-generated content published under Youthfront's name
  * AI-enabled impersonation of a Youthfront leader, staff member, or the organization
  * Voice cloning or a deepfake targeting Mike King or another Youthfront leader (a real risk given their public speaking and publishing footprint)
  * Exposure of Sensitive or Confidential data -- **especially any children 's data** -- through a tool that did not meet the Rules
  * A significant violation of the Policy by a staff member, volunteer, or contractor
  * AI-enabled fraud aimed at Youthfront's finances (for example, a fraudulent wire-transfer request appearing to come from a leader)
