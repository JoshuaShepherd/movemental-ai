FOR CHURCHES AND MINISTRIES

The church must decide what AI can do, and what it must never replace.

AI is already changing how sermons are prepared, people receive care, volunteers communicate, and churches organize their work.

The question isn’t simply which tools are useful. It’s what faithful leadership, genuine presence and human responsibility require as those tools become more capable.

Responsible for your church? Assess where your church stands

Your church is already making decisions about AI.

  • A pastor uses a model to help organize sermon research.
  • A ministry coordinator drafts communications with it.
  • An administrator considers using it to process prayer requests.
  • A volunteer tests an automated response system.

Not all of these uses are wrong. But they raise different questions of permission, disclosure, care and responsibility.

Right now, most churches are answering those questions one person at a time, without saying so.

The Map, applied to a church

The same five stages every organization walks, with the questions a congregation carries.

  1. 1Decide what you will stand for

    A church AI charter addressing preaching, pastoral care, confidentiality, disclosure and accountability, signed by your leaders and affirmed by your elders or board.

  2. 2Gather what should ground the work

    Appropriate teaching archives, administrative processes, policies and ministry knowledge, with prayer requests and pastoral conversations kept out.

  3. 3Practice the judgment your people will need

    Formation for pastors, staff, elders and volunteers, led by someone in your church.

  4. 4Build only what belongs

    Discern whether to implement research tools, ministry administration or specific workflow assistance, and refuse what doesn’t belong.

    PursuePursue carefullySolve simplyDeferRefuse

    An example refusal, written into the charter

    We will not substitute automated conversation for pastoral care in situations requiring personal discernment.

  5. 5Review what changes

    Revisit decisions as pastoral needs, staff practices and AI capabilities change.
