The nonprofit Map

The same five stages every organization walks, with the responsibilities a nonprofit carries for the people it serves.

  1. 1Decide what you will stand for

    Adopt boundaries for donor data, client information, disclosure, intellectual property and human responsibility.

  2. 2Gather what should ground the work

    Reconstruct approved organizational knowledge: programs, policies, reporting, grant history and institutional memory.

  3. 3Practice the judgment your people will need

    Develop staff judgment around accuracy, privacy, equity and responsible changes to how work is done.

  4. 4Build only what belongs

    Evaluate reporting tools, grant research, administrative automation or source-grounded assistants, and refuse what doesn’t belong.

    PursuePursue carefullySolve simplyDeferRefuse

    An example refusal, written into the charter

    We will not answer families automatically after hours. A person will always be the one who responds.

  5. 5Review what changes

    Revisit the effects on staff, the people you serve, costs, accountability and mission outcomes.

Efficiency creates choices.

When a process becomes faster, your organization has several legitimate options. None of them is automatic.

Which one belongs is an organizational decision, and it belongs in your charter.

Before

Program staff spend six hours each quarter drafting one grant report.

→

After

A drafting assistant and a person’s review take two.

The time that comes back could go to

More service for the people you serve
Lower operating costs
Investment in your staff’s development
Time returned to people
