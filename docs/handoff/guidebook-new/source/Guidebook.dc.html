Policy: what people may and may not do

A policy is the part of the charter people will actually consult. It answers a practical question for someone at a desk on an ordinary Tuesday: may I do this with AI, here, with this information?

Write it from what your people resolved and what the Reality Assessment found. A policy that ignores how people already work will be ignored in return.

Most good policies do three things. They name what is allowed, so people stop guessing. They name what is never allowed, with the reason, so the line holds under pressure. And they say who to ask when something falls in between.

Start with what is never allowed

Begin with the refusals, because they are the hardest to agree on and the easiest to lose. A refusal is a commitment, and you will cite it when a vendor or a deadline argues for an exception.

Then say what is allowed

Name the tools people may use and the kinds of work they may help with. Be specific enough that someone new could follow it on their first day.

Say who decides the rest

Something will always fall between the lines. Name the person or small group who answers those questions, and how quickly they will answer. Their decisions go into the Rules at the next review.

Do this now

Open Policy in your Map. The agent drafts from your people’s Resolutions and marks every line by where it came from. Settle the refusals first, then the rest.
